# GS Paper II — Resource Map (Mains)

Scope of this file: Mains GS Paper II only (Governance, Constitution, Polity, Social Justice and International Relations). Prelims-level chapter maps for Polity live in `references/polity.md`; marks, paper pattern and eligibility live in `references/exam-facts.md`. This file tells Claude what to read for Mains depth, what UPSC has actually asked (2013-2024), how to structure and grade an answer, and what a working professional should do tonight.

Paper shape (typically; verify the current year's notice and question paper at upsc.gov.in): 250 marks, 3 hours, 20 compulsory questions — 10 of 10 marks (answer in about 150 words) and 10 of 15 marks (about 250 words), printed in both Hindi and English. This 10+10 split has been the practice since 2017; 2013 had 25 questions of 10 marks and 2014-16 had 20 questions of 12.5 marks. Questions are statement-led ("Comment", "Discuss", "Critically examine", "Elucidate", "Analyse"), often quote a recent event, judgment or report, and reward — in roughly this order — a constitutional provision, a judgment or committee, a balanced critique and a way forward.

## Syllabus, decoded

Weight is qualitative ("high" = a question most years, often two; "medium" = roughly one every year or two; "low" = occasional). Years are from the GS II question papers; where the memory of a year is uncertain the theme is given without a year. All paraphrases are condensed; read the original wording on upsc.gov.in before quoting a PYQ to a learner.

### 1. Indian Constitution — historical underpinnings, evolution, features, amendments, significant provisions and basic structure
- Examiner wants: a reading of the Constitution as a living document — provisions, their purpose, how courts have read them, and whether amendments respect the basic structure. Not history of the Constituent Assembly for its own sake.
- Weight: high (basic structure, Art 21 expansion and the Preamble recur).
- PYQ themes: Supreme Court as a check on Parliament's arbitrary amending power (2013); judicial activism from the invention of the basic structure doctrine onwards (2014); factors that inhibit a uniform civil code under the DPSP (2015); right to a clean environment and regulating crackers under Art 21 (2015); what was held in I.R. Coelho and judicial review as a basic feature (2016); each adjective attached to "Republic" in the Preamble (2016); how "temporary" Art 370 really was (2016); whether Parliament under Art 368 can enlarge its limited amending power into absolute power (2019); constitutional morality through judicial decisions (2021); constitutional perspectives of gender justice with provisions and case law (2023); Constitution as a "living instrument" with reference to the expanding right to life and liberty (2023); privacy under Art 21 and DNA testing of a child in the womb to establish paternity (2024); environmental protection read into the Constitution by the courts (theme, year uncertain).

### 2. Functions and responsibilities of the Union and the States; issues and challenges of the federal structure; devolution of powers and finances to local levels and challenges therein
- Examiner wants: federalism as it works — Art 356, Governors, GST Council, Finance Commission, CSS design, local bodies' funds-functions-functionaries ("3Fs"); Sarkaria/Punchhi; S.R. Bommai.
- Weight: high.
- PYQ themes: federalism leaning towards a strong Centre despite being a basic feature (2014); cooperative federalism and drawbacks of the existing structure (2015); 69th Amendment and the Delhi LG-elected government conflict (2016); whether the July 2018 Supreme Court judgment settles the Delhi tussle (2018); cooperation, competition and confrontation shaping Indian federalism with recent examples (2020); centralising tendencies seen through the Epidemic Diseases Act 1897, Disaster Management Act 2005 and the farm acts (2020); CBI jurisdiction and whether states' power to withhold consent is absolute (2021); national parties favouring centralisation vs regional parties' autonomy (2022); legal and political reasons for reduced use of Art 356 since the mid-1990s (2023); 13th Finance Commission and local finances (2013); panchayats' own sources of finance beyond grants (2018); 14th FC and states' fiscal position (2021); states' reluctance to empower urban local bodies functionally and financially (2023); local bodies and good governance, pros and cons of merging rural and urban local bodies (2024).

### 3. Separation of powers between various organs; dispute redressal mechanisms and institutions
- Examiner wants: India's "checks and balances" model (Ram Jawaya Kapur), judicial legislation and PIL, ordinances, tribunals, Lok Adalats, arbitration, Inter-State Council, water-dispute tribunals, judicial independence.
- Weight: medium-high.
- PYQ themes: inter-state water dispute mechanisms failing — structural or process inadequacy (2013); ordinances and separation of powers, whether the power should be repealed (2015); the 2015 Arbitration and Conciliation ordinance and dispute resolution (2015); tribunals curtailing ordinary courts' jurisdiction, their validity and competence (2018); India follows checks and balances, not strict separation (2019); judicial legislation vs PILs seeking guidelines to the executive (2020); constitutionally guaranteed judicial independence as a prerequisite of democracy (2023); growth of PIL and whether the Supreme Court is the world's most powerful judiciary (2024); Lok Adalats vs arbitration tribunals, civil and criminal (2024).

### 4. Comparison of the Indian constitutional scheme with that of other countries
- Examiner wants: feature-by-feature comparison with a reason for India's choice, not a list of "borrowed features".
- Weight: medium (roughly one question in many years since 2018).
- PYQ themes: judicial systems of India and the UK converging and diverging (2020); Right to Equality in the USA and India (2021); election of the Presidents of India and France (2022); parliamentary sovereignty, British vs Indian (2023); India and the USA as two large democracies — similarities and differences in their federalism (theme, year uncertain).

### 5. Parliament and State legislatures — structure, functioning, conduct of business, powers and privileges and issues arising out of these
- Examiner wants: whether Parliament actually holds the executive accountable — committees, anti-defection, Speaker's office, Rajya Sabha's value, privileges, Legislative Councils, Governor's role in legislation.
- Weight: high (almost every year).
- PYQ themes: anti-defection law and the diminished role of individual MPs (2013; again 2019); codification of parliamentary privileges under Art 105 (2014); Estimates Committee and the usefulness of committees (2018); Rajya Sabha from "useless stepney tyre" to useful organ (2020); "Once a Speaker, always a Speaker" (2020); Departmentally Related Standing Committees keeping the administration on its toes (2020); Parliament's ability to ensure executive accountability (2021); Legislative Councils — provisions and working (2021); Vice-President as Chairman of Rajya Sabha (2022); Governor's legislative powers and re-promulgation of ordinances (2022); presiding officers of state legislatures and impartiality (2023); structure of the committee system and the financial committees' role in institutionalising Parliament (2023).

### 6. Structure, organisation and functioning of the Executive and the Judiciary; ministries and departments; pressure groups and formal/informal associations and their role in the polity
- Examiner wants: President/Governor discretion, cabinet system, collegium vs NJAC, judicial appointments and accountability, pressure-group methods.
- Weight: high for judiciary/executive; medium for pressure groups.
- PYQ themes: President's delay on mercy petitions and a time limit (2014); executive clemency as principle, not privilege (2014); cabinet size "as big as governmental work justifies" (2014); NJAC judgment and appointment of judges (2017); scope of Fundamental Rights after the privacy judgment (2017); pressure groups as the informal face of politics (2013); Khap panchayats as extra-constitutional authorities (2015); Indian party system in transition (2016); farmers' organisations' methods to influence policy (2019); business associations and public policy (2021).

### 7. Salient features of the Representation of the People's Act
- Examiner wants: RPA 1950/1951 provisions (1951: Sections 8, 29A, 62(5), 77, 123, 126), election petitions, ECI's powers and reforms (Vohra, Goswami, Indrajit Gupta, 255th Law Commission, ECI's 2016 proposals), simultaneous elections, EVM trust, Model Code of Conduct.
- Weight: medium-high (nearly every alternate year).
- PYQ themes: ECI's 2016 electoral reform proposals (2017); simultaneous elections — cost vs accountability (2017); EVM controversy and ECI's challenges (2018); simplifying the procedure for disqualifying persons guilty of corrupt practices under the RPA (2020); Model Code of Conduct and ECI's evolving role (2022); election disputes, grounds to declare an election void and remedies under RPA 1951, with case law (2022); electoral reforms suggested by committees, with reference to "one nation, one election" (2024).

### 8. Appointment to various constitutional posts; powers, functions and responsibilities of various constitutional bodies; statutory, regulatory and various quasi-judicial bodies
- Examiner wants: a body's constitutional/statutory basis, how its independence is secured, its performance, and reform.
- Weight: high (CAG, Finance Commission, NHRC, ECI, NCW, NCBC recur).
- PYQ themes: NHRC as a complement to the judiciary (2014); independence and autonomy of regulatory institutions (2015); what a quasi-judicial body is, with examples (2016); CAG's audit of policy implementation and Art 149 (2016); NCW's effectiveness (2017); CAG's appointment, terms and powers (2018); Finance Commission's constitution and the then newly constituted (15th) FC's terms of reference (2018); whether NCSC can enforce reservation in religious minority institutions (2018); whether to merge the many commissions for vulnerable sections into one Human Rights Commission (2018); Human Rights Commissions failing against the mighty — structural and practical limitations (2021); NCBC after becoming a constitutional body (theme, year uncertain).

### 9. Government policies and interventions for development in various sectors and issues arising out of their design and implementation
- Examiner wants: design flaws (targeting, leakage, Centre-state incentives, CSS rigidity), implementation evidence (CAG, NITI evaluations, surveys), and reform.
- Weight: high.
- PYQ themes: restructuring of Centrally Sponsored Schemes for the common man (2013); electronic cash transfer for welfare schemes (2013); Aadhaar vs NPR, voluntary vs compulsory (2014); a Rail Tariff Authority and regulatory reform, with the power-sector experience (2014); replacing price subsidy with DBT (2015); multi-level planning in the neo-liberal paradigm (2019); NEP 2020 and SDG-4 (2020); DBT as a progressive step with limits (2022); development schemes for the vulnerable being "discriminatory in approach" (2023).

### 10. Development processes and the development industry — the role of NGOs, SHGs, various groups and associations, donors, charities, institutional and other stakeholders
- Examiner wants: SHG-bank linkage economics, NGO regulation (FCRA 2010 amended 2020), civil society as alternative delivery, philanthropy/CSR.
- Weight: medium (SHGs appear often).
- PYQ themes: SHGs facing socio-cultural hurdles in rural areas (2014); SHG-Bank Linkage Programme as India's own innovation (2015); NGOs in environmental protection and their constraints (2015); recent changes in rules on foreign funding of NGOs under FCRA (2015); SHGs and the withdrawal of the state (2017); micro-finance as an "anti-poverty vaccine" and SHGs' twin objectives (2020); breaking the gender inequality-poverty-malnutrition cycle through micro-financing of women's SHGs (2021); civil society and NGOs as an alternative delivery model (2021).

### 11. Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections by Centre and States; performance of these schemes; mechanisms, laws, institutions and bodies for the protection and betterment of these sections
- Examiner wants: who the vulnerable are (SC/ST/OBC, women, children, elderly, disabled, transgender, minorities, migrants), the legal architecture (PCR Act 1955, PoA Act 1989, RPwD Act 2016, Transgender Persons Act 2019, POCSO 2012, MWPSC Act 2007, NFSA 2013), schemes and their evaluations.
- Weight: high.
- PYQ themes: whether protective schemes lead to exclusion of backward communities from urban business (2014); National Child Policy provisions and implementation (2016); poverty alleviation programmes as "show pieces" without political will (2017); RPwD Act 2016 as an effective mechanism for empowerment (2017); awareness and participation gaps in welfare delivery (2019); women's reservation in local bodies and patriarchy (2019); patriarchy persisting despite women's achievements — interventions beyond education and schemes (2021); welfare plus deft management of inflation and unemployment (2022); gig economy and women's empowerment (2024).

### 12. Issues relating to development and management of the social sector/services relating to Health, Education, Human Resources
- Examiner wants: public health system design (primary care, HWCs/Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, NHM, PMJAY), out-of-pocket spending, NEP 2020, higher-education quality, skilling-employment linkage, demographic dividend.
- Weight: high (at least one question most years).
- PYQ themes: autonomy for IITs/IIMs in courses and admissions (2014); foreign universities and higher-education quality (2015); private sector in bridging the universal health coverage gap (2015); demographic dividend remaining theoretical without skills (2016); Amartya Sen's reforms in primary education and health (2016); community-level health intervention for "Health for All" (2018); digital initiatives in the education system (2020); primary health structure as a precondition for sustainable development (2021); population education objectives (2021); education-skill-employment linkages (2023); the state's role against marketisation of healthcare at the grassroots (2024); inadequate attention to human resource development (theme, year uncertain).

### 13. Issues relating to poverty and hunger
- Examiner wants: measurement (Tendulkar, Rangarajan, national and global MPI), hunger indices with caveats, NFSA/PDS, divergence between poverty and hunger, nutrition programmes.
- Weight: medium.
- PYQ themes: mid-day meal scheme — twin objectives, mandates and success (2013); whether all poverty estimates show reduction, urban vs rural (2015); hunger and poverty as governance challenges (2017); growing divergence between poverty and hunger and shrinking social expenditure (2019); high growth with low human development (2019); the UN Multidimensional Poverty Index and incidence vs intensity (2020); poverty-malnutrition vicious cycle and human capital formation (2024).

### 14. Important aspects of governance, transparency and accountability, e-governance — applications, models, successes, limitations and potential; citizens charters, transparency and accountability and institutional and other measures
- Examiner wants: RTI (and the 2019 amendment), Lokpal, citizens' charters (2nd ARC, Sevottam), e-governance stack (Aadhaar, DBT, UMANG, CPGRAMS, DigiLocker), digital divide, social audit, whistleblower protection.
- Weight: high.
- PYQ themes: citizens' charters without improvement in satisfaction (2013); Section 66A of the IT Act and Art 19 (2013); the 2015 Whistleblowers amendment bill leaving "no one left to protect" (2015); LPG reforms and governmental responsiveness (2016); decline of public morality and Transparency International's index (2016); traditional bureaucratic structure hampering development (2016); citizens' charter as instrument of transparency and its limits (2018; 2024); e-governance and the "use value" of information (2018); factors that make ICT projects fail (2019); RTI Amendment 2019 and Information Commissions' autonomy (2020); digital illiteracy and ICT access vs development (2021); inadequacies hampering e-governance (2023).

### 15. Role of civil services in a democracy
- Examiner wants: neutrality, anonymity, politicisation, lateral entry, Mission Karmayogi, 2nd ARC 10th report, T.S.R. Subramanian judgment, Hota and Surinder Nath committees.
- Weight: medium (one question every two or three years; overlaps GS IV).
- PYQ themes: cadre-based civil service organisation as a cause of slow change (2014); "institutional quality drives economic performance" — civil service reforms to strengthen democracy (2020); public perception of civil servants' integrity and democratic governance (2024).

### 16. India and its neighbourhood — relations
- Examiner wants: country-specific stakes (Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Bhutan, Myanmar, Afghanistan), the "Neighbourhood First" frame, connectivity, Chinese presence, domestic politics in the neighbour.
- Weight: high (one or two questions every year).
- PYQ themes: ISAF withdrawal from Afghanistan and India's interests (2013); domestic factors in India-Sri Lanka relations (2013); Gujral doctrine and its relevance (2013); Shahbag protests and India (2013); political developments in Maldives (2013); South China Sea and India-China bilateral issues (2014); soft power in India-Pakistan ties (2015); Pakistan's conduct and the future of SAARC (2016); China's economic tools and military power — impact on India (2017); Maldives' geostrategic importance, trade and energy flows, maritime security (2024).

### 17. Bilateral, regional and global groupings and agreements involving India and/or affecting India's interests
- Examiner wants: grouping's origin, India's gains and constraints, and India's role; also big-power relations (US, Russia, Japan, EU, Israel, West Asia, Africa).
- Weight: high.
- PYQ themes: NDB and AIIB roles and strategic significance (2014); India's growing interest in Africa (2015); Project Mausam (2015); Look East policy's economic and strategic dimensions (2016); energy security and West Asia (2017); India-Israel ties that "cannot be rolled back" (2018); India-Japan global and strategic partnership (2019); friction in India-US ties (2019); Quad turning from security dialogue towards a trade bloc (2020); Indo-US vs Indo-Russian defence deals and the Indo-Pacific (2020); SCO's aims and importance for India (2021); US facing an existential challenge from China (2021); I2U2 (2022); "virus of conflict" in SCO (2023); NATO expansion and India (2023); India's relations with Central Asian Republics (2024); the West fostering India as a China alternative (2024).

### 18. Effect of policies and politics of developed and developing countries on India's interests; Indian diaspora
- Examiner wants: trade policy (WTO, tariffs, sanctions), climate finance, migration policy, diaspora as asset and liability.
- Weight: medium (diaspora roughly every three years).
- PYQ themes: tied aid conditions of international funding agencies (2014); Information Technology Agreements and India (2014); WTO Doha round deadlock (2016); WTO reform amid trade war (2018); US-Iran nuclear pact controversy and India (2018); India's image as leader of the oppressed vs its new role (2019); India's changing climate posture in global fora (2022); diaspora in South-East Asia (2017), in America and Europe (2020), scaling new heights in the West (2023).

### 19. Important international institutions, agencies and fora — structure, mandate
- Examiner wants: mandate, decision-making, critique (funding, politicisation), India's stance and reform proposals (UNSC, WTO, WHO, IMO, UNESCO, IMF/World Bank).
- Weight: medium.
- PYQ themes: WTO's mandate, binding decisions and India's food-security stand (2014); ECOSOC's functions and functional commissions (2017); UNESCO starved of cash and the US withdrawal (2019); WHO's role in global health security during Covid-19 (2020); IMO's role in environment, safety and security (2023).

Tonight (2 hours): pick one line above, read the PYQ themes, write one 150-word answer from memory on the oldest theme, then read the source rows for that line below and rewrite the answer with a provision, a judgment/committee and a way forward.

## Answer frameworks that work in GS II

Every GS II answer: a two-line introduction that defines or situates (constitutional article, judgment, report or datum), a body in 2-4 sub-headed parts matching the question's verbs, and a two-line conclusion that is a way forward or a balanced verdict. 150 words = intro 25, body 100, conclusion 25; 250 words = intro 35, body 180, conclusion 35. Underline the article, case and committee names.

### 1. Polity: provision -> issue -> judicial view -> committee recommendation -> way forward
Use for Governor, Art 356, ordinances, anti-defection, Speaker, privileges, tribunals.
Worked skeleton — "Discuss the legality of re-promulgation of ordinances by the Governor without placing them before the legislature" (2022):
- Provision: Art 213 — Governor may promulgate when the legislature is not in session and immediate action is necessary; the ordinance ceases six weeks after the legislature reassembles unless approved.
- Issue: repeated re-promulgation without laying before the House (the Bihar pattern of 1967-81 documented in D.C. Wadhwa); bypasses legislative scrutiny; blurs separation of powers.
- Judicial view: D.C. Wadhwa (1987) — re-promulgation as a routine is a "fraud on the Constitution"; Krishna Kumar Singh (2017, seven judges) — laying the ordinance before the House is mandatory, re-promulgation is a subversion of the democratic legislative process, the Governor's satisfaction is justiciable, and a lapsed ordinance creates no enduring rights unless public interest demands.
- Commission view: the Sarkaria and Punchhi reports treat the Governor's legislative powers as exceptional and advise sparing use; verify the exact paragraph before quoting, or use the Krishna Kumar Singh bench as the authoritative "expert view" slot.
- Way forward: mandatory laying with reasons for urgency, time-bound summoning of the House, legislative committee review of ordinances, publication of ordinance data by state legislatures.

### 2. Schemes: objective -> features -> performance data -> gaps -> recommendations
Use for any flagship scheme, DBT, CSS restructuring.
Worked skeleton — "Evaluate PM-JAY in enhancing the reach of public healthcare":
- Objective: financial protection for roughly the bottom 40% of the population against catastrophic hospitalisation, in line with National Health Policy 2017's aim of reducing out-of-pocket expenditure.
- Features: Rs 5 lakh per family per year, cashless, portable, public and empanelled private hospitals; 2024 extension to all citizens aged 70 and above regardless of income.
- Performance: cite the latest National Health Authority dashboard numbers (Ayushman cards issued, hospital admissions authorised) and the National Health Accounts estimate of the falling OOPE share in total health expenditure (verify the current figure before quoting).
- Gaps: skewed towards tertiary and private providers, weak primary-care linkage, fraud and package-rate disputes, state opt-outs, CAG performance audit (2023) findings on beneficiary database errors.
- Recommendations: integrate with Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, strengthen district hospitals, outcome-linked empanelment, grievance redressal, independent evaluation.

### 3. Bilateral relations: interests -> irritants -> cooperation areas -> way forward
Use for every neighbour and major-power question.
Worked skeleton — "Maldives' geostrategic importance for India" (2024):
- Interests: sea lanes carrying a large share of India's trade and energy pass near the Maldivian atolls; the Eight Degree Channel between Minicoy and the Maldives; security of the Indian Ocean region; Indian diaspora, tourism and medical travel.
- Irritants: "India Out" campaign (2023-24), withdrawal of Indian military personnel (completed 2024), Chinese FTA and debt exposure, Chinese research vessels calling at Male.
- Cooperation: Greater Male Connectivity Project, currency swap and budget support, Colombo Security Conclave, SAGAR/Neighbourhood First, hydrography, disaster relief (Operation Cactus 1988, the 2014 Male water crisis, Covid vaccines).
- Way forward: avoid zero-sum framing, deliver projects on time, people-centred ties, quiet security cooperation, insulate the relationship from domestic politics on both sides.

### 4. Governance: cause -> effect -> reform
Use for citizens' charters, e-governance, RTI, corruption, civil service.
Worked skeleton — "Why citizens' charters have not reached their potential" (2024):
- Causes: top-down drafting without consultation; no statutory backing (the Right of Citizens for Time Bound Delivery of Goods and Services and Redressal of their Grievances Bill, 2011 lapsed); vague standards; no penalty for breach; low awareness; charters not updated.
- Effects: no measurable improvement in satisfaction; grievances route to CPGRAMS or courts instead; charters become wall posters.
- Reform (2nd ARC 12th report): Sevottam model (charter + grievance redressal + capability building), citizen participation in drafting, measurable standards, periodic evaluation, state public services guarantee acts as a template.

### 5. Institution evaluation: mandate -> working -> critique -> reform
Use for NHRC, CAG, ECI, Finance Commission, NCW, Lokpal, CBI, Parliament committees.
Worked skeleton — "Human Rights Commissions have failed to assert themselves against the mighty" (2021):
- Mandate: Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 (amended 2006 and 2019); inquire, recommend, intervene in court proceedings, review safeguards, promote human-rights literacy.
- Working: large caseload, custodial death monitoring, advisories during Covid; quote the latest NHRC annual report for cases registered and compliance with relief recommendations rather than a remembered figure.
- Critique (structural): recommendations not binding; against the armed forces it can only seek a report from the Central Government (Section 19); one-year limitation on complaints (Section 36(2)); dependence on government-deputed staff; vacancies. (Practical): backlog, limited field presence, SHRCs weaker still.
- Reform: binding compliance reporting with reasons for rejection, own investigative cadre, broader membership (the 2019 amendment partly did this), funding autonomy, time-bound appointments.

### 6. Comparison questions: dimension table -> why India chose differently -> relevance today
Use for US/UK/France comparisons.
Worked skeleton — "Parliamentary sovereignty, British and Indian" (2023):
- Dimensions: source of authority (uncodified constitution and Parliament supreme vs written Constitution supreme), limits (none in law in the UK vs Arts 13, 368 and the basic structure in India), judicial review (no striking down of Acts in the UK, only declarations of incompatibility under the Human Rights Act 1998 vs full judicial review in India), federal limits (unitary vs the Seventh Schedule), amendment (ordinary law vs Art 368 majorities and ratification).
- Why India chose: federal and rights-based design after colonial experience; the Constituent Assembly settled on constitutional supremacy with harmony between organs rather than US-style judicial supremacy or UK-style parliamentary supremacy (Minerva Mills on harmony).
- Relevance: Kesavananda, NJAC and ordinance cases show the balance in practice; the UK's Brexit litigation (the Miller cases, 2017 and 2019) shows even UK courts now police executive-legislature boundaries.

### 7. Multilateral grouping: origin and aims -> India's stakes -> frictions -> India's role
Use for SCO, Quad, BRICS, G20, I2U2, ASEAN, BIMSTEC, international organisations.
Worked skeleton — "'Virus of conflict' in the SCO; India's mitigating role" (2023):
- Origin and aims: 2001, Shanghai Five plus Uzbekistan; security ("three evils" of terrorism, separatism, extremism), connectivity, economy; India and Pakistan joined 2017, Iran 2023, Belarus 2024.
- India's stakes: Central Asia access, counter-terrorism (RATS), energy, a non-Western forum where India sits with China and Russia.
- Frictions: India-China border, India-Pakistan terrorism, China's BRI endorsement that India declines, Russia-Ukraine war polarisation, Iran sanctions.
- India's role: 2023 chairmanship (virtual summit), proposals on terrorism, the "SECURE" formulation (2018), connectivity via Chabahar and INSTC rather than BRI, new pillars proposed under India's chairmanship (startups and innovation, traditional medicine, digital inclusion, youth, shared Buddhist heritage); keep issue-based engagement without expecting a security alliance.

### 8. Social sector / vulnerable group: status (data) -> causes -> state response -> gaps -> way forward
Use for women, children, SC/ST, disabled, elderly, health, education, hunger.
Worked skeleton — "Interventions beyond education and schemes to change patriarchal attitudes" (2021):
- Status: NFHS-5 (2019-21) — sex ratio at birth 929; about 29% of ever-married women aged 18-49 report spousal violence (verify against the fact sheet); female labour force participation low though rising (PLFS, cite the latest round).
- Causes: son preference, dowry, unpaid care burden, mobility restrictions, media representation, weak enforcement of the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005 and the Dowry Prohibition Act 1961.
- State response: Arts 14, 15(3), 16, 39(a) and (d), 42, 51A(e); Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, Mission Shakti, one-third reservation in panchayats (73rd Amendment), the 106th Amendment 2023 (women's reservation in Lok Sabha and assemblies, to take effect after the delimitation that follows the next census).
- Gaps: attitudes not addressed by transfers alone; "proxy" representation; low conviction rates; care economy ignored.
- Way forward: gender-responsive budgeting with outcome audit, boys' engagement curricula, paid care infrastructure (creches), time-use surveys, community-level role models, enforcement data published.

### 9. Statement questions: decode -> agree with evidence -> counter -> verdict
Use whenever the question is a quotation ending in "Comment"/"Discuss"/"Elucidate".
Worked skeleton — "Rajya Sabha has turned from a 'useless stepney tyre' to a useful organ" (2020):
- Decode: "stepney" = spare wheel, used only in emergencies (the Constituent Assembly's doubts about a second chamber).
- Agree: federal voice (Arts 249, 312), revising chamber that delayed or amended bills (Lokpal select committee, land acquisition amendment 2015, GST consensus 2016), select committees, continuity, expertise through nominated members.
- Counter: money-bill route bypasses it (Aadhaar Act 2016; the money-bill question referred to a larger bench in Rojer Mathew 2019), domicile requirement removed in 2003 (upheld in Kuldip Nayar 2006), disruption, party whips reduce its federal character.
- Verdict: usefulness is real but conditional on restraint in money-bill certification and on states' voice; suggest a limited reform (e.g., defined criteria for money bills).

## Data, examples, judgments and committees to keep ready

### Landmark Supreme Court judgments (one-line holdings)
1. A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras (1950) — "procedure established by law" read literally; no due process.
2. Shankari Prasad (1951) and Sajjan Singh (1965) — Parliament may amend Fundamental Rights; "law" in Art 13 excludes constitutional amendments.
3. Golaknath (1967) — Fundamental Rights cannot be amended.
4. Kesavananda Bharati (1973) — amending power is wide but cannot alter the basic structure.
5. Shamsher Singh (1974) — President and Governor are bound by ministerial advice except in constitutionally recognised discretion.
6. Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975) — free and fair elections, rule of law and judicial review are basic features; clause (4) of Art 329A inserted by the 39th Amendment struck.
7. Maneka Gandhi (1978) — Art 21 procedure must be just, fair and reasonable; Arts 14, 19 and 21 read together.
8. Minerva Mills (1980) — limited amending power and FR-DPSP harmony are basic features; Art 368(4) and (5) struck.
9. Waman Rao (1981) — Ninth Schedule additions after 24 April 1973 are open to basic-structure review.
10. Olga Tellis (1985) — right to livelihood is part of Art 21.
11. Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano (1985) — maintenance under Section 125 CrPC applies irrespective of personal law.
12. D.C. Wadhwa (1987) — routine re-promulgation of ordinances is a fraud on the Constitution.
13. Indra Sawhney (1992) — 27% OBC reservation upheld; creamy layer excluded; 50% ceiling; no reservation in promotions under Art 16(4) (Art 16(4A) added by the 77th Amendment 1995).
14. Kihoto Hollohan (1992) — Tenth Schedule valid; Speaker's decisions subject to judicial review.
15. Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Assn (1993, Second Judges) and the 1998 Presidential Reference (Third Judges) — collegium system; NJAC case (2015) struck down the 99th Amendment and the NJAC Act.
16. Unni Krishnan (1993) — education up to 14 years is a fundamental right under Art 21 (basis of the 86th Amendment 2002).
17. S.R. Bommai (1994) — Art 356 proclamations are justiciable; floor test is the test of majority; secularism is a basic feature.
18. L. Chandra Kumar (1997) — tribunal decisions are subject to High Court review under Arts 226/227; judicial review is basic structure.
19. Vishaka (1997) — guidelines against sexual harassment at the workplace (replaced by the POSH Act 2013).
20. Vineet Narain (1997) — directions for CBI and CVC autonomy.
21. PUCL v. Union of India (2001 onwards) — right to food; mid-day meals and PDS directions.
22. Prakash Singh (2006) — seven police-reform directives (State Security Commission, fixed tenure, separation of investigation from law and order, Police Complaints Authority).
23. M. Nagaraj (2006) and Jarnail Singh (2018) — reservation in promotions needs quantifiable data on inadequacy of representation and efficiency; Jarnail dropped the backwardness-data requirement and applied the creamy layer to SC/ST in promotions.
24. Kuldip Nayar (2006) — removal of the domicile requirement and open ballot for Rajya Sabha elections upheld.
25. Rameshwar Prasad (2006) — Bihar assembly dissolution without a floor test unconstitutional.
26. I.R. Coelho (2007) — Ninth Schedule laws added after 24 April 1973 are reviewable if they damage the basic structure.
27. Raja Ram Pal (2007) — expulsion of MPs for cash-for-query within the House's privilege, but judicially reviewable.
28. Ashoka Kumar Thakur (2008) — 93rd Amendment and OBC reservation in central educational institutions upheld, creamy layer excluded.
29. Lily Thomas (2013) — Section 8(4) RPA struck; immediate disqualification on conviction.
30. PUCL (2013) — NOTA option on EVMs.
31. T.S.R. Subramanian (2013) — fixed tenure for civil servants, Civil Services Boards, written instructions from the political executive.
32. NALSA (2014) — transgender persons' right to self-identified gender; third gender recognised.
33. Manoj Narula (2014) — no bar on ministers facing criminal charges; PM expected to exercise constitutional trust.
34. Shreya Singhal (2015) — Section 66A of the IT Act struck for vagueness and chilling effect.
35. Rajbala v. State of Haryana (2015) — educational and other qualifications for panchayat candidates upheld.
36. Nabam Rebia (2016) — Governor cannot summon or advance the session on his own; the Speaker-removal aspect was referred to a larger bench in 2023.
37. Abhiram Singh v. C.D. Commachen (2017) — appeal to religion, caste, language of candidate or voter is a corrupt practice under Section 123(3) RPA.
38. Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar (2017) — ordinance must be laid before the House; re-promulgation is subversion; satisfaction is justiciable.
39. K.S. Puttaswamy (2017) — privacy is a fundamental right under Art 21; (2018) Aadhaar Act upheld with conditions, Section 57 struck, passage as a money bill upheld 4:1.
40. Shayara Bano (2017) — instant triple talaq unconstitutional.
41. Common Cause (2018) — passive euthanasia and advance directives permitted.
42. Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India (2018) — LG bound by aid and advice except land, police and public order; (2023) services under the elected government; followed by the GNCTD (Amendment) Act 2023.
43. Navtej Singh Johar (2018) — Section 377 IPC decriminalised for consenting adults; Joseph Shine (2018) — adultery (Section 497 IPC) struck.
44. Indian Young Lawyers Assn (Sabarimala, 2018) — entry of women of all ages; review questions referred to a nine-judge bench (2019).
45. Rojer Mathew (2019) — Tribunal rules 2017 struck; money-bill question referred to a larger bench.
46. Anuradha Bhasin (2020) — internet access is linked to Art 19; shutdown orders must be proportionate and reviewable.
47. Keisham Meghachandra Singh (2020) — Speaker should decide disqualification petitions within about three months; Parliament urged to rethink the Speaker as adjudicator.
48. Janhit Abhiyan (2022) — 103rd Amendment (EWS reservation) upheld.
49. In re Article 370 (2023) — abrogation upheld; statehood to be restored and elections directed.
50. Anoop Baranwal (2023) — ECI appointment by a committee of PM, LoP and CJI until Parliament legislates (Parliament then passed the CEC and Other ECs Act 2023 with a Union Cabinet Minister in place of the CJI).
51. Subhash Desai (Shiv Sena, 2023) — Governor's floor-test call wrong; Speaker to decide disqualification in reasonable time; Nabam Rebia referred to a larger bench.
52. State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to the Governor (2023) — Governor cannot withhold assent indefinitely under Art 200; must return the bill to the House.
53. Supriyo v. Union of India (2023) — no fundamental right to marry; same-sex marriage left to the legislature.
54. Association for Democratic Reforms v. Union of India (2024) — Electoral Bond Scheme struck as violating Art 19(1)(a).
55. Sita Soren v. Union of India (2024) — P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998) overruled; no immunity for bribery under Arts 105/194.

## What UPSC asked, 2013-2024 (patterns)

- A quoted statement to "Comment"/"Discuss" is the dominant form; roughly half the paper is statement-led, so the decode-agree-counter-verdict skeleton is used every year (2016 party system; 2019 "leader of the oppressed"; 2020 Rajya Sabha; 2021 US-China; 2023 "living instrument"; 2024 "the West fostering India").
- Basic structure and judicial review are asked in some form every two or three years (2013, 2014, 2016 Coelho, 2019 Art 368, 2020 judicial legislation, 2023 judicial independence, 2024 PIL).
- Art 21's expanding frontier is the favourite Fundamental Rights theme: crackers and a clean environment (2015), privacy (2017), living instrument (2023), DNA testing and privacy (2024), environmental protection through the courts (year uncertain).
- Parliament's accountability function appears almost every year through a different door: anti-defection (2013, 2019), privileges (2014), committees (2018, 2020, 2023), Speaker (2020), Rajya Sabha (2020), Vice-President (2022), presiding officers (2023).
- Governor and Centre-state friction is a recurring pair: Delhi LG (2016, 2018), centralising laws (2020), CBI consent (2021), Governor's ordinances (2022), regional parties (2022), Art 356 decline (2023).
- Local government questions alternate between finance (2013 13th FC, 2018 own sources, 2021 14th FC) and effectiveness/empowerment (2015 panchayats as political institutions, 2017 local self-government, 2019 women's reservation, 2023 ULBs, 2024 merger of rural and urban bodies).
- Electoral reform is asked roughly every alternate year and usually names the ECI or a committee (2017 ECI proposals and simultaneous elections, 2018 EVMs, 2020 corrupt-practice disqualification, 2022 MCC and election petitions, 2024 "one nation one election").
- Constitutional bodies are asked with their Article and independence provisions: CAG (2016, 2018), Finance Commission (2018), NHRC (2014, 2021), NCW (2017), NCSC (2018), merging commissions (2018), regulators (2015).
- Comparison questions since 2018 name a specific country and a specific feature: judiciary UK (2020), equality USA (2021), President France (2022), parliamentary sovereignty UK (2023).
- Social justice questions pair a scheme or law with a design critique: CSS restructuring (2013), Aadhaar (2014), DBT for subsidies (2015), poverty programmes (2017), awareness and participation (2019), NEP and SDG-4 (2020), DBT (2022), "discriminatory in approach" (2023).
- SHGs and NGOs are asked every two or three years with the economics of micro-finance or regulation (2014, 2015, 2017, 2020, 2021).
- Health and education come most years, usually asking for a primary-level or structural remedy, not scheme lists (2015, 2016, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2023, 2024).
- Governance questions re-use the same instruments: citizens' charters (2013, 2018, 2024), e-governance and ICT (2018, 2019, 2021, 2023), RTI (2020), whistleblowers (2015), civil services (2014, 2020, 2024).
- IR questions are of three kinds: a neighbour at a moment of change (Maldives 2013 and 2024, Bangladesh 2013, Afghanistan 2013, Sri Lanka 2013, SAARC 2016), a grouping and India's role (NDB/AIIB 2014, Quad 2020, SCO 2021 and 2023, I2U2 2022, NATO 2023, Central Asia 2024), and a great-power framing (US 2019, Japan 2019, Russia defence 2020, US-China 2021, "the West" 2024).
- International institutions are asked through a crisis of the moment: WTO Doha and food security (2014, 2016, 2018), ECOSOC (2017), UNESCO funding (2019), WHO and Covid (2020), IMO (2023).
- Diaspora recurs roughly every three years (2017 South-East Asia, 2020 America and Europe, 2023 the West) and expects both economic (remittances, investment) and political (lobbying, soft power) benefits with a line on risks (Gulf labour conditions, extradition disputes, dual-loyalty allegations).

## Teaching and grading in this paper

### Teaching a GS II topic in a 60-90 minute slot (weeknight: 2 hours = one slot plus one answer)
1. Frame (5 min): state the syllabus line, the weight, and read out two PYQs on the topic so the learner knows the destination.
2. Provision and concept (15-20 min): the Articles or Act sections close to verbatim, the purpose, and the one comparison (UK/US) if relevant. Deliver the content in the chat; do not point to the book without saying what it says.
3. Evolution and contest (15-20 min): the two or three judgments and the committee recommendations, in date order, each in one line with the holding; the current controversy or news hook up to 2024.
4. Data and examples (10 min): one statistic with source and year, one Indian example, one state example (the learner's home state where possible).
5. Build the answer (15-20 min): pick one PYQ, agree on the framework from the list above, write the skeleton together (headings plus one line each), then the learner writes 150 or 250 words in the remaining time or tonight.
6. Close (5 min): a one-page note template — Provision | Issue | Judgments | Committees | Data | Way forward — and the next PYQ to attempt cold. On weekends (7 hours) run three such slots plus two timed answers and one evaluation.

For working professionals (about 24 h/week across all papers): a GS II syllabus line averages two slots; if GS II gets four slots a week, the 19 lines above take about 10 weeks; IR topics are revised monthly from the newspaper, not re-taught. A full-timer can run two GS II slots a day alongside another paper and finish the lines in four to five weeks, then move to weekly timed sectional tests.

### Grading a GS II answer (score out of 10 or 15; report the band)
Check in this order and quote the learner's own sentences when deducting:
1. Demand decoded: did the answer address every verb and clause ("examine", "legality", "suggest")? A missed clause typically costs a substantial share of the marks; say which clause.
2. Constitutional provision cited: at least one Article/Schedule/Section correctly stated and correctly used; a wrong article number is worse than none.
3. Judgment or committee named: at least one relevant case or commission with its holding in a line; for schemes, one evaluation source (CAG, NITI, Parliamentary Standing Committee, NFHS).
4. Balanced critique: both the structural and the practical side; for a statement question, both agreement and counter; no editorialising without evidence.
5. Data or example: one figure with source, or one concrete instance; flag any invented or stale figure.
6. Way forward: specific and sourced (an ARC/Punchhi/Law Commission line, a court's direction, a state's model), not "government should take steps".
7. Structure and word discipline: intro-body-conclusion, sub-headings, within about 10% of the word limit, underlining of key terms; budget about 7 minutes for 150 words and about 10-11 minutes for 250 words so that 20 answers fit in 180 minutes with reading time.
8. IR-specific: interests and irritants both present, India's agency stated (what India can do, not only what it faces), and no outdated status (check the latest joint statement).

Bands (an internal mentoring scale, not an official UPSC scale; UPSC publishes no marking scheme): 0-3 generic prose without provision or example; 4-5 provision present but one-sided or no way forward; 6-7 provision + judgment/committee + balance + way forward; 8+ all of the above with a precise datum, a comparison or a current hook and tight structure. Always end an evaluation with a rewrite of the weakest section in the target word count, then set tonight's task: the same question again from memory in 8 minutes, and one new PYQ from the same syllabus line.
