# The Full-Cycle Roadmap — Prelims to Mains to Interview

Use this file with `scripts/make_roadmap.py` (it prints the dated phases for a given start date, target year and weekly hours). This file supplies the reasoning behind those phases, the three starting points, what carries over between stages, the two waiting gaps, the repeat-attempt diagnosis, and the checkpoints to use in reviews. Prelims chapter maps live in the subject files; Mains and Interview detail lives in `mains-overview.md`, `gs1.md`–`gs4-ethics.md`, `essay.md`, `answer-writing.md`, `optional-subjects.md` and `interview.md`.

Every date below is the *typical* position of the UPSC calendar. Dates move (the 2024 Prelims was shifted from 26 May to 16 June because of the general election); the official calendar and notification at upsc.gov.in override anything here. Cutoffs, vacancies and the exact windows for DAF-I and DAF-II change every year; never quote a figure from memory as fixed.

Notation used throughout: "P" is the Prelims month, "M" the Mains month; "P-4" means four months before Prelims; **"P+1" means the day after the Prelims paper**, not the next month.

## The cycle in one table

| Event | Typical timing | Gap from the previous event | What is being prepared in this window |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notification + online application (optional subject, medium, Paper A language and Mains centre are declared in the form and cannot be changed later) | Typically late January to mid-February; the window stays open about three weeks | — | Prelims foundation should already be finished or in its last subject; the optional decision is forced here |
| Prelims (GS Paper I 9:30–11:30 and CSAT 2:30–4:30, same day, typically a Sunday) | Typically late May / early June | ~15 weeks after the notification | Revision 2–4 and full-length mocks; 2–3 weeks of leave for a working professional |
| Prelims result (roll-number PDF on upsc.gov.in) | Typically 2–3 weeks after Prelims (mid/late June; early July in 2024) | 2–3 weeks | **Mains sprint starts the day after Prelims**, not here; the result only confirms the direction. DAF-I window opens soon after the result and is short |
| Mains (9 papers on 5 exam days spread over about 10 calendar days — typically Friday to Sunday, then the following weekend; Essay on day 1, GS I–II day 2, GS III–IV day 3, Paper A and B day 4, optional day 5) | Typically mid/late September | ~14–16 weeks after Prelims (roughly 100 days), ~12–13 weeks after the result | GS answer writing, optional Paper II, essay practice, Paper A/B, 2 full Mains tests; 4–6 weeks of leave |
| Mains result | Typically early/mid December | ~10–12 weeks | DAF-II filing (window opens within days of the result), DAF-based question bank, home-state and hobby prep; next-cycle Prelims revision in parallel if the attempt is uncertain |
| Personality Test (Interview), UPSC, Dholpur House, Shahjahan Road, New Delhi, in batches | Typically January to April | 4–16 weeks after the result, depending on the batch allotted | Mocks, current affairs of the last 6 months, opinions file; about a week of leave including travel |
| Final result | Typically April / May, within days to about three weeks of the last board | — | Service and cadre allocation follow (DoPT); UPSC then publishes the Prelims answer keys, the cutoffs and the marks of candidates who were not recommended — use them for the diagnosis below |

Rough arithmetic for a learner who starts cold: Prelims to final result is ~11 months, so a start 18–24 months before Prelims means roughly 29–35 months from the first study day to the final result.

## Three starting points

Hours arithmetic used throughout: working professional (WP) = 5 weeknights × 2 h + 2 weekend days × 7 h = **24 h/week ≈ 100 h/month**; full-timer (FT) = 6 days × ~8 h + a lighter Sunday ≈ **55 h/week ≈ 240 h/month**. Public holidays count as extra Saturdays. Leave figures are what to request, not what is guaranteed; ask for them early. A week of leave converts five 2-hour nights into five ~8-hour days, i.e. roughly +30 h for that week.

### Starting point 1: 18–24 months before Prelims (foundation for Prelims + Mains together)

Principle: read every GS source once in a form that serves both stages (Prelims facts + Mains dimensions in the same notes), take the optional's first reading before Prelims, and start answer writing in month 6 (2 answers/week), scaling to 10/week by month 9. Months are counted from the first study day.

| Phase | Months (24-month start) | WP (24 h/wk) | FT (55 h/wk) | What gets done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Foundation I | 1–6 | Weeknights: one chapter + its PYQs; Saturday: long chapters + CSAT 1.5 h; Sunday AM: weekly test; from month 6, 2 GS answers/week | Mornings: 5 h main subject; afternoons: PYQs, notes, second subject; Sunday: test + backlog | Polity (Laxmikanth, *Indian Polity*, whole), Modern History (Spectrum, *A Brief History of Modern India*), Geography (NCERT XI *Fundamentals of Physical Geography* and *India: Physical Environment*, XII *India: People and Economy*, + atlas), Economy (NCERT XII *Introductory Macroeconomics* + XI *Indian Economic Development*). Class VI–XII NCERTs for History/Geography in the FT version. One newspaper daily (≤ 1 h) with an examples bank from day one. Optional shortlist + 2-week trial in months 4–6 (`optional-subjects.md`); declare it only at the form |
| B. Foundation II + answer writing | 7–12 | Tuesday/Thursday nights = 2 answers each; Sunday 2 h for 3 answers; by month 9: 10 answers/week | 10 answers/week from month 7; 1 essay/fortnight from month 9 | Environment (Shankar IAS *Environment*), Art & Culture (Nitin Singhania *Indian Art and Culture*, selected chapters), S&T (NCERT + current affairs), Ancient/Medieval via PYQs; Mains-only GS: GS I society + world history, GS II governance/IR, GS III security + agriculture, GS IV theory with thinkers (`gs4-ethics.md`). Optional Paper I first reading (WP: every Saturday 3 h + one weeknight; FT: 2 h daily). Prelims sectional tests monthly |
| C. Optional II + Mains tests + Prelims revision 1 | 13–16 (P-11 to P-8) | Optional Paper II on Saturdays; essay fortnightly; one Mains sectional test/month | Optional Paper II; one Mains full-length GS test every 3 weeks; essay weekly | Finish the optional syllabus once with PYQs mapped; first Mains test series (sectional); Prelims revision 1 from notes, subject by subject; CSAT PYQ paper to diagnose |
| D. Prelims mode | 17–20 (P-7 to P-4) | 24 h; weekly GS tests; answer writing down to 3/week | 55 h; two full-length Prelims mocks a week from P-5 | Prelims revision 2 and 3, current-affairs compilation (last 12–15 months), CSAT weekly if the diagnostic was below 80; optional only as weekly revision of notes |
| E. Mocks | 21–24 (P-3 to P, the final ~12 weeks) | 24 h + **2–3 weeks leave** before the exam (~+30 h/week in those weeks) | 55 h | Full-length mocks at 9:30 AM, analysis, revision 4, no new sources; see `plan-template.md` Phase "Mocks" |
| F. Mains sprint | P+1 to Mains (~100 days) | **4–6 weeks leave** before Mains; until then 24 h with 15+ answers/week | 55 h; 20 answers in 3 h by week 8 | `mains-overview.md` *The 100-day sprint*; optional revision 2; essay weekly; Paper A/B one weekend each |
| G. DAF and Interview | Week after Mains to the board | 10–15 h/week while waiting; 24 h from the result | 30 h/week; mocks weekly from the result | `interview.md`; DAF-II, hobby/state/degree bank, mocks |

With 18 months instead of 24: compress A to 4 months (skip the Class VI–X NCERT pass for a graduate who read them at school; use the PYQ-driven approach for Ancient/Medieval), run B as 5 months (answer writing from month 5), and shorten C to 2 months; D (4 months) and E (3 months) are unchanged. Do not shorten D or E.

WP leave plan for this path: 2–3 weeks before Prelims, 4–6 weeks before Mains, 5–7 days around the Interview. Tell the learner to raise it with their manager 6 months ahead and to bank leave through the year; many take the Mains block as unpaid leave or a sabbatical.

### Starting point 2: 7–12 months before Prelims (Prelims-first, Mains-aware reading)

Principle: the Prelims decides whether Mains exists, so the source list is Prelims-trimmed (`plan-template.md`), but notes are taken in Mains form (every topic gets its "why it matters / what has gone wrong / what to do" line and one example). The optional is studied before Prelims only if **more than 9 months** remain; otherwise it is chosen at the form and studied after Prelims.

| Phase | Months | WP (24 h/wk) | FT (55 h/wk) | What gets done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Foundation (first reading) | 1 to ~55% of the time (e.g. months 1–6 of 11) | Weeknight chapter + PYQs; Saturday long chapters + CSAT; Sunday test | 5 h main subject + 1 h PYQs + 1 h revision daily | Polity, Modern History, Geography, Economy, Environment, then A&C, S&T, Ancient/Medieval via PYQs; current affairs daily; CSAT PYQ paper in month 1 to diagnose |
| B. Mains-aware additions | from month 2, ~2 h/week | 2 GS answers/week (150 words) on the chapter just read | 4 answers/week + 1 essay/month | Keeps the hand warm without eating Prelims time; grade with the `answer-writing.md` rubric |
| C. Optional (only if > 9 months remain) | from month 3 to P-4 | Saturday 3 h + one weeknight = ~5 h/week | 2 h daily | Paper I core topics with PYQs; stop at P-4 wherever you are |
| D. Revision + sectional tests | ~20% of the time | 24 h; one sectional test/week | 55 h; two sectional tests/week | Second pass from notes; subject-wise PYQs in one sitting; current-affairs compilation |
| E. Mocks | last 10–12 weeks (~25%) | 24 h + 2–3 weeks leave | 55 h | Full-length mocks, analysis, revisions 3–4; CSAT weekly |
| F–G | as in Starting point 1 | | | Mains sprint from P+1; DAF prep from the week after Mains |

At exactly 7 months, run the Starting point 3 phases but keep the Mains-form note-taking. At exactly 9 months, do the optional's 2-week trial but no further study of it before Prelims.

### Starting point 3: fewer than 7 months before Prelims (Prelims only)

Principle: nothing but Prelims until the exam. The optional is *chosen* (2-week trial if the form deadline allows; otherwise by background, syllabus length and GS overlap from `optional-subjects.md`) and declared in the form, but not studied. Mains answer writing starts at P+1.

| Phase | Months | WP (24 h/wk) | FT (55 h/wk) | What gets done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Foundation | first ~55% (e.g. months 1–3.5 of 6) | `assets/plan-16-weeks-working-professional.json` mapped to dates by `scripts/make_plan.py`; Saturday CSAT track | Same chapter order at ~2.5× pace; add Class XII Geography and Singhania in full | Trimmed source list only (`plan-template.md`); PYQs after every chapter; CSAT diagnostic in week 1 |
| B. Revision | next ~20% | Notes-based second pass; one sectional test/week | Two sectional tests/week | Current-affairs compilation (one monthly magazine, last 12 months) |
| C. Mocks | last 8–10 weeks (~25%) | 24 h + **2–3 weeks leave**; a mock every Sunday at 9:30 AM, two a week in the last 4 weeks | 55 h; 2–3 mocks/week | Analysis sheet per mock: error type (knowledge / misread / over-attempt); accuracy target ≥ 70% on attempted |
| D. Mains sprint | P+1 to Mains | 4–6 weeks leave; optional first reading now, in parallel with GS answer writing | 55 h | `mains-overview.md`; the optional is the biggest risk on this path — say so plainly and prefer an optional with GS overlap |

With fewer than 4 months, say the honest thing: it is a long shot for a WP unless leave is available for most of the final 6 weeks; the attempt still has value as a rehearsal if attempts are not scarce (check `exam-facts.md` for attempts and age; appearing in either Prelims paper consumes an attempt). With fewer than 3 months and scarce attempts, advise skipping the attempt and running Starting point 1 for the next cycle.

## What transfers between stages

Read every source once, in a way that serves three stages. The table says where a Prelims subject lands in Mains and what the Interview board tends to do with it.

| Prelims subject | Mains paper and part | Interview angle | Example of the same topic across stages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polity | GS II (Constitution, federalism, Parliament/judiciary, bodies, governance); GS IV (constitutional morality, civil-service values) | Your opinion on contested constitutional questions; your state's politics and Governor–government relations | Prelims 2021 asked which option best describes the status of the right to property (a legal right under Art. 300A, available to any person, not a fundamental right); GS II 2023 quoted "the Constitution of India is a living instrument with capabilities of enormous dynamism" and asked for illustration with reference to the expanding horizons of the right to life and personal liberty; a board may ask whether judicial expansion of Art. 21 has gone too far |
| Modern History | GS I (freedom struggle, personalities, post-independence consolidation) | Freedom fighters and movements of your home state/district; your view on a contested historical figure | GS I 2022 asked why the East India Company's armies, mostly Indian soldiers, won consistently against the more numerous and better-equipped armies of Indian rulers |
| Art & Culture, Ancient & Medieval | GS I (Indian culture: art forms, literature, architecture from ancient to modern times) | Your hobby if it is a performing/visual art; your state's dance, temple style, festival, GI-tagged crafts | Prelims asks temple styles, sects and texts; GS I asks what a style or school reveals about its society; boards ask "what is special about the temples of your district" |
| Geography | GS I (physical geography, resources, urbanisation, climate phenomena); GS III (disaster management) | Your state's rivers, crops, minerals, disaster profile; a district-level problem you would solve as DM | GS I regularly asks the mechanism of a phenomenon (monsoon, tropical cyclones, ocean currents) and its effect on India; boards ask about flooding or drought in your home district |
| Economy | GS III (growth, budgeting, agriculture, MSP, industry, infrastructure, inclusive growth) | Your sector or employer's economics; Budget and Economic Survey opinions; your state's GSDP and its weak sectors | Prelims 2020 asked which step an RBI adopting an expansionist monetary policy would *not* take (raising the MSF rate); GS III 2022 asked whether inclusive growth is possible under a market economy and the significance of financial inclusion for growth |
| Environment | GS III (conservation, pollution, EIA, climate change) | Local environmental conflict (mining, dam, wetland, urban air) and your position on it | Prelims asks protected areas, IUCN status, conventions; GS III asks policy trade-offs; boards ask "development vs environment in your state" |
| Science & Technology | GS III (achievements of Indians, indigenisation, IT/space/biotech/IPR) | Your degree subject applied to governance ("how would an engineer use this in a district?") | Prelims asks the term in the news; GS III asks its application and governance issues; boards ask the ethics of the same technology |
| Current affairs | Every GS paper's data and examples; Essay content | Your opinions file; last 6 months before the board | A scheme appears as a Prelims statement, as a GS answer example, and as a board question on its design flaw |
| CSAT | Paper B (English comprehension and précis), Essay logic, reading speed in GS papers | Clarity and structure of spoken answers | Reading-comprehension practice carries into reading 20 Mains questions correctly in the first 10 minutes |

**How to read once and use thrice.** For every chapter: (1) Prelims layer — facts, numbers, exceptions, in a fact table; (2) Mains layer — one line each for *significance, issues, government response, way forward*, with one example or figure; (3) Interview layer — a one-sentence opinion if the topic is contested. Write (2) and (3) only for topics on the Mains syllabus; skip them for pure Prelims trivia.

**Notes to keep from day one** (one file each, kept short, revised monthly):

- *Examples bank* (Mains): scheme or initiative + what it achieved or where it failed + one number. Example entries: PM-KISAN, MGNREGA, Jal Jeevan Mission, Swachh Bharat Mission, Aspirational Districts Programme, PMJDY, DBT, Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY; state innovations (Kerala's Kudumbashree, Odisha's Mission Shakti SHG programme and Mo Sarkar, Tamil Nadu's noon-meal scheme) are worth more than central ones because fewer candidates use them.
- *Data sheet* (Mains): one authoritative figure per theme with the source — Economic Survey, Union Budget, PLFS annual report, NFHS-5 (2019–21), Census 2011, NCRB *Crime in India*, India State of Forest Report, ASER, NITI Aayog indices (SDG India Index, National MPI). Never a figure you cannot source. **Update every figure to the latest edition available at the time of the Mains**; as of 2024, NFHS-5 and Census 2011 were still the latest rounds.
- *Judgments and committees* (GS II/IV): Kesavananda Bharati (1973), Maneka Gandhi (1978), Minerva Mills (1980), Indra Sawhney (1992), S.R. Bommai (1994), Vishaka (1997), Lily Thomas (2013), the NJAC judgment (Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association, 2015), Shreya Singhal (2015), Puttaswamy (2017), Shayara Bano (2017), Navtej Singh Johar (2018), Joseph Shine (2018); Sarkaria (1988) and Punchhi (2010) commissions on Centre–state relations, the 2nd ARC's fifteen reports, the Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990) on electoral reforms, the Vohra Committee (1993) on the criminal–politician nexus. One line each: what was decided or recommended, and what it is used to argue.
- *Quotes and thinkers* (Essay, GS IV): 30 quotes you actually understand and can attribute correctly; thinkers' one-liners from `gs4-ethics.md`.
- *Opinions file* (Interview): one short paragraph per contested issue (reservation, Uniform Civil Code, simultaneous elections, death penalty, freebies, AI and jobs, federalism, your sector's regulation) — your position, the strongest counter, and your reply to it.
- *State and district file* (Interview): Governor, Chief Minister, number of districts, major rivers, crops, minerals, industries, GSDP sector shares, flagship state schemes, three live problems, three things the state does well, your district's history and economy. Refresh the office-holders and figures in the month before the board.
- *DAF facts* (Interview): everything you write in the DAF must be defensible for 10 minutes — hobby, prizes, employer, college, service preference order, cadre preference. Keep the draft from month 1 and edit it as you go.

## The Prelims-Mains gap (100 days)

The detailed sprint — week-by-week, paper by paper, test schedule, Paper A/B weekends — is in `mains-overview.md` *The 100-day sprint*. This section is the decision on the evening of the Prelims.

**Decision tree, Prelims evening (after checking two or three coaching answer keys; UPSC publishes its own key and the cutoffs only after the final result):**

1. Compute the net GS Paper I score = (correct × 2) − (wrong × 0.667), and the CSAT score (+2.5 per correct, −0.833 per wrong; 66.67 needed to qualify). Compare the GS-I net with the published cutoffs of the previous two or three years for your category, taken from upsc.gov.in; cutoffs move every year with the paper's difficulty, so treat last year's figure as a reference, not a line.
2. **CSAT below 66.67 on every key** → the attempt is over; take two days off, then run the *Repeat attempts* diagnosis below and start the next cycle's Starting point 1 or 2 with CSAT as the first fix.
3. **GS-I clearly above the recent cutoffs (by 10+ marks on every key) and CSAT clear** → full Mains sprint from the next morning; arrange the Mains leave now.
4. **Borderline (within roughly ±8–10 marks of the recent cutoffs, or keys disagree)** → **start the Mains sprint anyway.** Waiting 2–3 weeks for the result costs roughly 15–20% of the sprint; preparing for a Mains you do not write still yields a head start on next year's Mains and a finished optional. There is no scenario where waiting wins. Keep one weekly Prelims-revision slot (2 h, PYQs only) as the hedge.
5. **Clearly below** → start the optional first reading and GS answer writing at a lighter 10–12 h/week for a WP (it is next year's head start), with Prelims revision resuming by August.

**Result day.** Check the roll number in the PDF on upsc.gov.in, not in a forwarded screenshot. If cleared: fill DAF-I when the window opens (typically within days of the result, open for about two weeks; the optional, medium, Paper A language and centre were fixed in the application, and the DAF must be consistent with it — employment, education and hobby entries become the Interview syllabus), confirm leave dates, and sort out Mains-centre logistics. If not cleared: close the Prelims notes for a week, keep the answer-writing habit at 3/week, and do the diagnosis in *Repeat attempts* within the fortnight, while the paper is fresh.

## The Mains-Interview gap

Interview preparation itself — what boards assess, the DAF as the syllabus, the 8–10 week plan, question types and the mock protocol — is in `interview.md`. This section is about the 10–12 weeks between the last Mains paper and the result.

**Week 1 after Mains:** rest; write down, paper by paper, how many questions you attempted and which you guessed, while you remember (this is the raw material for the diagnosis if the result is bad, and it fades within a month).

**From week 2, regardless of how Mains went:**

- Draft the DAF-II answers (service and cadre preferences with reasons; any updated employment details) and the *State and district file* and *Opinions file* if they do not exist. Everything in DAF-I is already on the record; preparing it is not optional.
- Read the newspaper for opinions, not facts: one hour, with a line per editorial in the opinions file.
- Reconnect with your hobby so you can talk about it from practice, not memory.

**If the attempt is uncertain (fewer than ~17–18 answers attempted in several GS papers, optional felt weak, or an essay went off-topic):** run the next cycle's Prelims revision 1 in parallel — 10–12 h/week for a WP (subject-wise PYQ sets from notes), 20 h/week for a full-timer — until the result. The next Prelims is ~5–6 months from the Mains result; a candidate who waits for a December result to restart has lost half the runway. Keep the DAF work at 4–5 h/week anyway; the board will not wait for a confident candidate.

**If the attempt felt strong:** DAF-based preparation at 10–15 h/week from the second week after Mains, interview mocks starting the week of the result. Still keep one Prelims PYQ set a week.

**On the Mains result:** if called, DAF-II is due within a short window after the result — have it drafted. Book travel to Delhi early for the allotted date; request 5–7 days of leave. If not called, UPSC publishes the marks of non-recommended candidates after the final result (typically within a few weeks of it, i.e. April–May); use them for the diagnosis below, but do not wait for them to restart — the next Prelims is ~24 weeks from the Mains result.

## Repeat attempts

The first question in a review of a failed attempt is *which stage failed and in what way*; the second is *what to change*; the third, which learners skip, is *what not to change*.

### Prelims failed

Reconstruct the paper from the question paper and an answer key: attempted, correct, wrong, net; CSAT separately. The thresholds below are working heuristics, not UPSC figures.

| Pattern | Diagnosis | Change |
|---|---|---|
| CSAT < 66.67 | CSAT, nothing else matters | CSAT PYQ paper every fortnight from month 1; reading-comprehension drills; quantitative basics from R.S. Aggarwal *Quantitative Aptitude* only for the failed topics (`csat.md`). Never again "I'll see CSAT in May" |
| Attempted ~85+ with accuracy < 65% | Over-attempting; elimination weak | Mocks with a hard cap of 80–85 attempts; the error sheet must classify every wrong answer (did not know / knew but misread / guessed against the rule); `mcq-style.md` elimination rules |
| Attempted < 70 with accuracy ≥ 70% | Coverage or confidence; net too low because of unattempted questions | Finish the trimmed source list, 4 revisions; raise attempts to 80+ in mocks by P-3; learn to take the 50:50 after elimination |
| Wrong answers cluster in one or two subjects | Subject gap | Re-read that subject's primary source; the rest stays as revision only |
| Wrong answers spread across current-affairs/scheme questions | Current affairs done from too many sources or too late | One monthly magazine, last 12–15 months, compiled by subject; stop the daily PDF collection |
| Net within ~5 marks of the published cutoff | No structural problem | Add revision count and mock count; change nothing else |

Do **not** change the books, the optional, or the weekly shape that got the learner close; change revision count, mock count and attempt discipline. A learner who switches sources after a near miss usually loses the next attempt as well.

### Mains failed (Prelims cleared)

Wait for the marksheet (published after the final result) but start the correction from the self-report written in the week after Mains.

| Pattern | Diagnosis | Change |
|---|---|---|
| All four GS papers low and similar | Generic answers: no structure, no examples, no data; or too few answers attempted | `answer-writing.md` method and templates; 10 answers/week graded against the rubric for 6 months; build to 20 answers in 3 hours in timed tests before P+1 |
| One GS paper far below the others | Paper-specific content gap (often GS I world history/society, GS III security/internal economy, or GS IV case studies) | That paper's file; one month of targeted reading + 2 timed tests; the rest remains revision |
| Essay far below the GS papers | Topic choice, structure or going off-topic | `essay.md`: topic-choice protocol, 2 timed essays/month graded, the examples bank |
| Optional papers low and the learner disliked studying it | Wrong optional | The only case for a switch, and only if ≥ 12 months remain to the next Mains; otherwise redo the same optional with PYQ mapping and a test series (`optional-subjects.md`) |
| Optional low but the learner likes it and reads it well | Under-practised: answer count, diagrams, PYQ-specific demands | Optional test series; 5 full papers before P+1; no source change |
| Fewer than ~17 answers attempted in a GS paper | Speed | Timed 10-marker drills (~7 minutes each), skeleton-first writing, word-count discipline |
| Failed Paper A or Paper B (25% qualifying) | Treated the qualifying papers as free | Two weekends on the paper's format; one timed past paper each from upsc.gov.in (`mains-overview.md` *Paper A and Paper B*) |
| Missed the Interview call by a narrow margin | Nothing structural | More tests, same sources; keep answer writing at 10/week outside the Prelims phase and 3/week minimum inside it |

Do **not** rewrite notes from new sources, add a second test series, or switch the optional on hearsay. Do keep the Prelims routine that worked: Mains-focused repeaters routinely fail the next Prelims because they drop the mock discipline.

### Interview not converted (final list missed)

The Interview score alone rarely decides; compare the Mains total too. If Mains was low, the fix is the Mains table above. If Mains was adequate and the Interview score was low: `interview.md` *Mock interview mode* with recorded mocks, work on brevity and on admitting ignorance cleanly, and a tighter DAF (fewer claims you cannot defend).

### Working-professional specifics

- **Leave strategy:** 2–3 weeks before Prelims and 4–6 weeks before Mains are the minimum; ask in writing six months ahead and bank leave. If both blocks are impossible in one year, protect the Mains block (the Prelims can be cleared on weekends and nights; 20 answers in 3 hours cannot be built on 2-hour nights).
- **When to consider a break from work:** after clearing Prelims and missing Mains or the Interview once or twice with a stable routine — the remaining gap is hours, not method. A 12-month break starting right after a Prelims (so it covers one Mains sprint, one Interview window and the next Prelims) is the highest-value window. Also when attempts are scarce (two or fewer left) and age allows one more cycle.
- **When not to:** on a first attempt (the diagnosis is not yet known), when the failure was CSAT or over-attempting (both are fixable in 24 h/week), when financial runway is under 18 months, or when the job itself is a strong DAF asset the board will ask about. Negotiate work-from-home, a reduced role, or unpaid leave blocks before resigning.
- **The routine fix comes before the hours fix:** a WP who did 24 h/week with no mocks should first do 24 h/week with mocks; only if that still falls short does more time help.

## Checkpoints Claude should use in reviews

Compare done vs due against these; report the gap in weeks and re-plan with `scripts/make_roadmap.py` and `scripts/make_plan.py`. Months are from the first study day; "P" and "M" are the Prelims and Mains months; "P+1" is the day after Prelims. Accuracy means correct ÷ attempted on a timed PYQ or sectional test. Score targets below are self-set training margins, not UPSC figures.

**Starting point 1 (18–24 months)**

- Month 1: CSAT PYQ paper taken (score recorded); newspaper routine fixed at ≤ 1 h; examples bank started; Laxmikanth up to *Fundamental Rights* with PYQs.
- Month 3: Polity and Modern History first reading done; 2 sectional tests at ≥ 60% accuracy; optional shortlist of 2–3 subjects.
- Month 6: Geography and Economy done; the 2-week optional trial completed and the subject chosen; 4 sectional tests, at least two at ≥ 65%; first 10 Mains answers written and graded (band recorded).
- Month 9: Environment, A&C, S&T done; 10 answers/week sustained for 8 weeks; one 150-word answer in under 8 minutes; optional Paper I half done with PYQs mapped; first essay written and graded.
- Month 12: optional Paper I done; GS IV theory and thinkers done; Mains sectional test on one GS paper taken (score and answer count recorded); Prelims sectional accuracy ≥ 65% across subjects.
- Month 16 (P-8): optional Paper II done; two Mains full-length GS tests done, at least one with 18+ answers attempted; essay fortnightly for 4 months (last two graded above the middle band); Prelims revision 1 done; current-affairs compilation current to last month.
