# Current Affairs & Government Schemes (method) — Resource Map

> Scope note: this is a METHOD subject. There is no single textbook. The "primary book" here is a bundle: (1) one monthly compilation (Vision IAS / InsightsIAS / ForumIAS — pick one), (2) the Economic Survey, (3) the Union Budget documents, fed daily by one newspaper + PIB and monthly by PRS. India Year Book is secondary and selective. The chapter map below covers all of these unit by unit.

## Weight and exam behaviour

- GS Paper I (100 Qs, 200 marks, 2 hours) is the only paper that matters here; CSAT is qualifying. Roughly 25-35 questions each year carry a current-affairs hook, but only about a third of those are "pure" current affairs (a scheme, a report, a place, a species, a summit, a space mission). The rest are static concepts that a news item made examinable (e.g. a Supreme Court order triggers a question on the underlying Article; an RBI announcement triggers a question on what SDF or the MPC is).
- Formats UPSC uses for this subject: (a) "With reference to X, consider the following statements… which of the statements given above is/are correct" (2-3 statements); (b) since 2022, "How many of the above statements are correct? Only one / Only two / All three / None", which removes option-elimination; (c) "Consider the following pairs: Scheme : Ministry / Place : Country / Exercise : Partner — how many pairs are correctly matched"; (d) "Which one of the following best describes the term 'X', recently seen in the news"; (e) since 2023, Statement-I / Statement-II with "does Statement-II explain Statement-I".
- Trend 2017-2024 (qualitative): 2017-2019 had many direct scheme and index-publisher questions (e.g. Digital India aims 2018; Atal Innovation Mission is under NITI Aayog 2019; Global Competitiveness Report publisher 2019). 2020-2021 shifted to concept-with-news-trigger, especially economy, environment and S&T, and fewer "name the ministry" questions. 2022-2024 brought the "how many" format, harder statements lifted almost verbatim from PIB releases and Economic Survey text, and a longer news window (items up to 18-24 months old appear). Species, places-in-news and international groupings have appeared every single year.
- News window to cover for a late-May Prelims: June of the previous year to April of the exam year as the core; for environment, IR groupings and S&T missions, go back 18-24 months.
- Negative marking is one-third of the marks for the question (0.66 of the 2 marks). Arithmetic that should decide every attempt: in a 2-statement question where you are certain about one statement, your guess is 50:50 and the expected value is +0.67 — attempt. In a 3-statement "how many" question where you know nothing for sure, the guess is 1-in-4 and the expected value is zero — skip. Current-affairs questions have the lowest accuracy of any area for most candidates; be stingier here than in Polity or Geography.
- Scheme questions are almost never about the scheme's name; they are about one design detail: ministry, year, central sector vs centrally sponsored, beneficiary definition, benefit quantum, exclusion clause, funding ratio. Learn schemes as a fixed set of fields (see "Scheme card" in the teaching template).

Sources to skip and why:
- Daily current-affairs videos / YouTube "daily news analysis" channels (45-60 min a day): the same content is in the monthly compilation in 10 minutes of reading; for a 2-hour evening this is the single biggest time sink.
- Reading two newspapers, or newspaper plus three Telegram PDF dumps: duplicates, no revision time left. One newspaper plus PIB is complete.
- Yearly 400-page "Prelims current affairs compilations" from four different institutes: read one institute's monthlies that you already made notes from; a second institute's yearly adds anxiety, not marks.
- Manorama Year Book cover-to-cover and India Year Book cover-to-cover: thousands of facts, very few questions.
- Editorial note-making for Prelims: editorials are opinion; Prelims tests facts and definitions.

## Chapter map

Priority legend: High = read fully and make the note; Medium = read, note only the tables; Skim = 15-30 min, headings and boxes; Skip = do not open for Prelims. "Night" = one weekday block of about 2 h; "half night" = about 1 h.

## How to teach a topic from this subject

When the learner says "teach me X" / "explain X" and X is a scheme, mission, report, grouping, Bill, index, species-in-news or Budget/Survey concept, follow this order and keep the whole answer to roughly one screen:

1. One-line definition and the news hook (what happened, when, approximately: month/year).
2. Basis and ownership: nodal ministry (and implementing agency if different), legal/constitutional basis if any (Act, Article, executive scheme), type (central sector / centrally sponsored with funding ratio; statutory / constitutional / executive body; binding / non-binding for international instruments).
3. Key facts table — the scheme card fields: launched (year), ministry, type, target beneficiary and eligibility, benefit quantum, exclusions, funding pattern, outlay/duration if stated, recent change. For a report/index: publisher, periodicity, components, what a high score means, India's direction. For a grouping: year, HQ/secretariat, members, India's status, latest summit. For a species: IUCN, WPA schedule, CITES, habitat/state, threat, why in news. For a Budget/Survey concept: formula, which document, Article/Act.
4. What UPSC has asked: name the nearest PYQ(s) with year and the exact angle (e.g. "PMFBY 2016 tested premium uniformity and post-harvest coverage"; "AIM 2019 tested the parent body"; "capital budget 2016 tested classification"). If there is no direct PYQ, say so and give the pattern UPSC would use.
5. Three traps: usually (a) the look-alike scheme/body/report with a different ministry or publisher, (b) year of launch vs restructuring/renaming, (c) a quantifier UPSC flips ("all", "only", "mandatory", "voluntary", "per person" vs "per household").
6. Two statement-style check questions in the current UPSC format (one 2-statement "which is/are correct", one 3-statement "how many are correct"), with answers and a one-line reason for each statement. Then ask the learner whether they want the adjacent static topic (e.g. after PM-JAY, offer "Ayushman Bharat vs NHM" or "Centrally Sponsored Scheme funding norms").

For "what exactly should I study tonight for current affairs": name the unit and row from the chapter map, the exact source (e.g. "Vision monthly, Environment section, species table" or "Economic Survey chapter on Prices and Inflation, boxes and tables only"), the time block, and the note to produce.

## Revision & test strategy

- Note format: one "CA master sheet" (spreadsheet or 8 tabs in a notes app), never a diary. Tabs: Schemes (card fields), Bodies/Bills, Groupings & summits, Reports & indices, Species & PAs, Places (map pins), S&T missions, Exercises & defence. One row per item, one line per field. Target size at exam time: under 60 pages equivalent. Economic Survey and Budget get two pages each (10-number sheet, deficits box, climate commitments, new announcements).
- Cadence for a working professional: daily 40 min (newspaper + PIB, marking only); two weekends a month at 2.5-3 h on Saturday and Sunday on the previous month's compilation sections (≈ 10-12 h), updating the master sheet; PRS Monthly Policy Review on the first weekend of the month (60-90 min); Economic Survey and Budget in the first two weekends of February; India Year Book marked chapters one night a week from March to August of the year before the exam. The remaining weekend hours stay with the static subjects.
- Revisions: each month's material is revised three times — (1) within the same month while filling the sheet, (2) at the end of each quarter by re-reading only the sheet (not the compilation), (3) in the final 6 weeks, two full passes of the sheet (one in April, one in the 10 days before the exam). Items you got wrong in tests get a mark and are revised a fourth time in the last 3 days.
- PYQs: do 2013-2024 GS Paper I. First pass by year, untimed, tagging every current-affairs question with the news month/year and the ministry/publisher; second pass of 2017-2024 only, timed, in the final two months. Use the tags to calibrate how old news can be and which ministries recur.
- Tests: from month 2 of preparation, one 30-40-question monthly current-affairs sectional from any one test series, taken the weekend after you finish that month's compilation. From February, one combined current-affairs test every two weeks (previous 8-10 months). From April, only full-length GS mocks, where CA questions are attempted using the expected-value rule (attempt when at least one option is eliminated; skip pure guesses in "how many" format). Analyse every wrong CA answer into one of four bins: not covered, covered but forgotten, misread quantifier, guessed; only the second bin goes back to the master sheet.
- Attempt discipline in the real exam: mark CA questions in three passes — sure, one-option-eliminated, blank. Return to the second group only after the static questions are done. Do not revise your "blank" decisions in the last five minutes.

## Linking current affairs to this subject

How a news item becomes a Prelims question: UPSC takes the item, strips the event, and tests (a) the design facts of the thing in the news (ministry, year, type, number, member list), (b) the static concept the item sits on (Article, Act, convention, scientific principle, economic definition), and (c) one deliberately altered quantifier. So for every item, write the news in one line, then the two or three static facts it rests on, then one "flip" statement you would use to trick yourself. Five worked conversions (all pre-2025):

1. News (Sept 2022): eight cheetahs from Namibia released in Kuno National Park. Conversion: "Consider the following statements: 1. Kuno National Park lies in the Chambal river basin in Madhya Pradesh. 2. The cheetah is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. 3. The cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952. How many are correct?" (Statement 2 is the flip: the species is Vulnerable; the Asiatic subspecies is Critically Endangered.) Static base: Kuno-Palpur as the proposed second home for Asiatic lions (Supreme Court order, 2013); IUCN categories; WPA 2022 amendment schedules.
2. News (June 2023): India signed the Artemis Accords during the PM's US visit. Conversion: "With reference to the Artemis Accords, consider: 1. They are a legally binding treaty negotiated under the UN Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space. 2. They are grounded in the Outer Space Treaty of 1967. 3. India is a signatory. Which is/are correct?" (Statement 1 is false: non-binding, NASA-led.) Static base: Outer Space Treaty 1967 (India party), Moon Agreement 1979 (India signed, not ratified), ISRO reforms (IN-SPACe, NSIL).
3. News (Nov-Dec 2022): RBI began wholesale and retail e₹ pilots. Conversion: "Which of the following statements best describes the Central Bank Digital Currency in India? (a) a cryptocurrency regulated by SEBI; (b) a digital form of legal tender issued by RBI and appearing as a liability on its balance sheet; (c) a UPI-based wallet run by NPCI; (d) a stablecoin pegged to the rupee issued by banks." Static base: RBI Act Section 22 (sole right to issue banknotes; amended via Finance Act 2022 to include digital form), legal tender vs deposit money, UPI vs CBDC.
4. News (17 Sept 2023): PM Vishwakarma launched. Conversion: "Consider the following pairs: PM Vishwakarma : Ministry of Rural Development; PM SVANidhi : Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs; PM-JANMAN : Ministry of Tribal Affairs. How many pairs are correctly matched?" (Only two: PM Vishwakarma is under the MSME ministry.) Second angle: "It is a centrally sponsored scheme with 60:40 funding" (false; central sector). Static base: central sector vs centrally sponsored classification; MSME definition 2020.
5. News (Sept 2023, G20 New Delhi): Global Biofuels Alliance and India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor launched; African Union admitted to G20. Conversion: "Consider: 1. India, Brazil and the USA are founding members of the Global Biofuels Alliance. 2. India's ethanol-blending target is 20% by Ethanol Supply Year 2025-26. 3. The African Union became a permanent G20 member at the Johannesburg BRICS summit. How many are correct?" (Statement 3 wrong venue.) Static base: G20 composition and presidency rotation (troika), National Policy on Biofuels 2018 (amended 2022, E20 target advanced to 2025-26), ISA as the model UPSC used in 2016 (launch venue and membership statements).

Two more quick patterns to recognise: an Economic Survey box (e.g. on "effective revenue deficit" or "core inflation") becomes a definition question the same year; a Budget Part A announcement (e.g. 2023-24 "MISHTI" for mangroves) becomes a "which ministry / what objective" pair-matching question the following year, often paired with an older look-alike (Green India Mission, Nagar Van, Amrit Dharohar).
