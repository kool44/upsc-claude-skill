# Science & Technology — Resource Map

## Weight and exam behaviour
- **Typical count:** roughly 6-12 questions out of 100 in GS Paper I; it swings widely year to year. Rough internal split in most years: biotech/health 3-5, space & defence 1-3, IT/emerging tech (AI, blockchain, 5G, quantum) 2-3, applied physics/chemistry 1-2. Treat these as tendencies, not quotas.
- **Formats used:** (a) "Consider the following statements… how many of the above are correct?" (introduced 2022; options "only one / only two / all three / none", which kills pair-elimination); (b) "What is X, often mentioned in the news?" / "The term X refers to" (Cas9 2019; Web 3.0 and NFT 2022; aerial metagenomics 2023; Wolbachia method 2023); (c) "X can do / is used in which of the following" application lists (RNAi 2019; AI 2020; Visible Light Communication 2020; carbon nanotubes 2020); (d) classification questions ("bacteria, fungi, virus — which can be cultured in artificial medium" 2021; "CCTV, RFID, WLAN — which are short-range technologies" 2022; "ballistic vs cruise missile; Agni-V vs BrahMos" 2023; "which of the above need an accelerometer" 2023).
- **Trend (~2016-2024):** 2016-2019 leaned on tech buzzwords (Li-Fi 2016, Project Loon 2016, AR/VR 2019, Cas9 2019; blockchain 2020). 2020-2023 shifted hard to biotech and health (germline gene editing 2020, pronuclear transfer 2020, mitochondrial replacement therapy 2021, Bisphenol A and Triclosan 2021, COVID vaccine platforms 2022, extremophiles 2023, Wolbachia 2023, probiotics 2023). Space and defence appear almost every year but usually 1-2 Qs. Pure NCERT physics/chemistry is rare but recurs at "application" level (isotopes, scattering, alloys, polymers).
- **The question is almost always "news term + NCERT mechanism".** UPSC names a current item, then tests the underlying Class 9-12 science (what a vector is, what a retrovirus is, how a vaccine type works, what a Lagrange point is).
- **Negative marking:** S&T has the highest share of "never heard of it" options. Rule: attempt only if you can eliminate two options outright, or in a 3-statement question you are sure of two statements. Realistic target for a working professional: attempt 5-7 of the S&T Qs at high accuracy; leave the rest. Do not chase the obscure 2-3 Qs by reading more; they are not where marks come from.

## Chapter map

**Time budget.** Static S&T gets **one week ≈ 24 h** (5 weeknights × 2 h + two weekend blocks ≈ 14 h). Priorities below are set to that budget:
- **High = core, inside the 1-week budget (≈ 23 h total: ≈ 20 h of chapters + ≈ 3 h on the "Mapping / data to memorise" lists).**
- **Medium = extension (≈ 15 h total).** Do these as 45-60 min slots in the revision months, one chapter per week alongside CA reading. Do not let them push the week-1 core.
- **Skim = 30-60 min, read once, no note unless stated. Skip = do not open.**
Read a chapter once, make the note in the last column, then never re-read the chapter — revise the note.

## Mapping / data to memorise

## How to teach a topic from this subject

When the learner says "teach me X" or "explain X", answer in this fixed order, in under ~500 words, then stop and ask whether they want depth or the next topic:

1. **One-line definition + one everyday analogy.** (e.g. "CAR-T: the patient's own T cells are re-engineered in a lab to carry a receptor that targets the cancer, then re-infused — a custom-trained security force.")
2. **Scientific basis (3-6 lines), anchored to the NCERT chapter it comes from.** Name the chapter ("this is *Human Health and Disease* → cell-mediated immunity, T cells"). Explain the mechanism at the level a Class 12 reader would follow; no equations.
3. **Key facts table (5-8 rows):** what it is | who/where developed | year | Indian institution/mission/scheme and ministry | agency or regulator | numbers only if official (range class, orbit, year, payload count) | status as of 2024.
4. **What UPSC has asked:** cite the PYQ with year and the exact angle (e.g. "2021: mitochondrial replacement therapy — tests whether it can prevent transmission of mitochondrial disease and whether mitochondrial disease comes only from the mother"). If nothing direct, give the nearest analogous PYQ and say so; never invent a PYQ.
5. **Three traps:** the confusions UPSC exploits — a look-alike term (RT-PCR vs PCR; isotope vs isobar), a wrong ministry/agency, a "first/only/all" absolute, or a mechanism reversal (vaccine = active immunity, not passive).
6. **Two statement-style check questions** in UPSC's current format (2-3 statements, "how many are correct"), with answers and one-line reasons given after the learner responds, not before.
7. **Add one line:** "Note to make: …" naming which table in the Resource Map this fits into (disease table, GM product table, mission list), so the learner files it rather than re-reads it.

For a mission/scheme (ISRO, DRDO, DBT, DST), replace step 2 with "Objective → implementing agency and ministry → year → components/payloads → what is new vs the previous version". For a disease, replace step 3's table with: pathogen class | vector/route | organ | diagnostic test | vaccine/treatment | India programme.

## Revision & test strategy
- **Three revisions, from notes only.** R1 within 7 days of finishing the core week (re-read your tables, not the chapters). R2 at the 30-45 day mark, combined with that month's CA S&T section and the extension chapters done since. R3 in the final 45 days before Prelims, using only the consolidated tables plus the annual CA compilation.
- **Short-note format:** one page per theme, tables not prose. The full set should be ~15 pages: organelles; enzymes/hormones (human + plant); diseases (pathogen class/vector/test/vaccine); immunity types; genetic disorders; biotech tools; GM products; microbes → products; vaccine platforms with Indian examples; ISRO vehicles + missions by year; DRDO missiles by type; nuclear three-stage + reactors; schemes with ministry and year; emerging-tech glossary with one-line mechanism; compounds/alloys/polymers.
- **PYQs:** do every S&T question from 2013-2024 (roughly 100 Qs). First pass in two sittings right after the core week, scoring honestly and tagging each Q as "static-NCERT", "CA-linked" or "obscure". Second pass in R3. Note the Q wording style: this is your model for converting news into questions.
- **Sectional tests:** one static S&T sectional after the core week (expect weak scores on tech buzzwords; that is normal); one CA-based S&T sectional per month from the magazine's test series; from 60 days before the exam, stop sectionals and rely on full-length mocks, reviewing only the S&T Qs you got wrong or guessed.
- **Attempt discipline in mocks:** log every S&T attempt as "knew", "eliminated to 2", "guessed". If "guessed" accuracy stays below the break-even level over three mocks, stop guessing in S&T entirely; the marks you save exceed the marks you gain.

## Linking current affairs to this subject

**Conversion rule:** news item → underlying NCERT mechanism (chapter) → Indian institution/ministry/year → the one "absolute" UPSC can test (first/only/all/none) → write it as a 3-statement question. If a news item has no NCERT mechanism behind it and no Indian agency, it is low-yield; note the name and move on.

1. **Chandrayaan-3 soft landing (Aug 2023)** → *Gravitation* (why a lander needs retro-propulsion to shed velocity) + ISRO launch vehicles. Likely Q: "Consider the following statements: 1. Chandrayaan-3 was launched by LVM3. 2. It landed near the lunar south pole. 3. The rover's LIBS instrument confirmed the presence of sulphur in the regolith. How many are correct?" (All three.) Trap: Chandrayaan-3 carried a propulsion module, not a full orbiter, and used the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter for communication relay.
2. **India's first CAR-T therapy NexCAR19 approved (2023)** → *Human Health and Disease* (T cells, cell-mediated immunity) + *Biotechnology: Principles and Processes* (gene insertion via viral vector). Likely Q: "With reference to CAR-T cell therapy: 1. It uses the patient's own immune cells. 2. It involves genetic modification of those cells. 3. It is currently used mainly for blood cancers." (All correct.) Trap: it is not a vaccine and not a small-molecule drug.
3. **GEAC recommends environmental release of GM mustard DMH-11 (2022)** → *Biotechnology and its Applications* (transgenic crops, selectable markers) + regulation. Likely Q: "1. DMH-11 uses the barnase-barstar system derived from a soil bacterium. 2. GEAC functions under the Ministry of Agriculture. 3. Bt cotton is the only GM crop under commercial cultivation in India." (1 and 3 correct; GEAC is under MoEFCC.)
4. **National Quantum Mission approved (2023)** → *Structure of the Atom* / *Semiconductor Electronics* vocabulary (qubit vs bit; superposition, entanglement). Likely Q: "Consider: 1. NQM is implemented by DST. 2. Its four verticals are computing, communication, sensing & metrology, and materials & devices. 3. Quantum key distribution is an encryption method that relies on classical computing." (1 and 2 correct; QKD relies on quantum properties of photons.)
5. **R21/Matrix-M malaria vaccine recommended by WHO (2023), made by Serum Institute** → *Human Health and Disease* (Plasmodium life cycle, Anopheles, active immunity) + vaccine platforms. Likely Q: "1. Malaria is caused by a virus transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes. 2. R21 is a protein-subunit vaccine with an adjuvant. 3. RTS,S was the first malaria vaccine recommended by WHO." (2 and 3 correct; malaria is a protozoan disease transmitted by female Anopheles.)
