# Indian Economy — Resource Map

## Weight and exam behaviour
- Typical load: roughly 12–18 questions out of 100 in GS Paper I, year to year. Nearly every question is answerable from a small conceptual core (money & banking, fiscal, external sector, national income, inflation indices) plus one layer of "what changed this year".
- Formats UPSC uses here: (a) 2–3 statement "which of the statements is/are correct" (most common); (b) "How many of the above…" (from 2023); (c) "Which of the following best describes the term X" (RBI/SEBI jargon: call money, SDF, CBDC, NEER/REER, NFT, interest coverage ratio); (d) cause→effect reasoning ("If RBI decides to adopt an expansionist monetary policy, which of the following would it NOT do?" — 2020; answer: increase the MSF rate); (e) list-matching of institutions / reports / indices with publishers; (f) occasional arithmetic on GDP deflator, money multiplier, or fiscal deficit = total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts).
- Trend over ~2016–2024: steady move from "what is the scheme / what year was X launched" towards "what does this instrument DO and who regulates it". Questions on RBI operations (repo corridor, OMO, SLR, PSL, UCBs, bond yields), payments (UPI/NPCI, payments banks), capital vs money market (2023), REITs/InvITs (2023), fiscal concepts (FRBM 2018, capital budget 2016), external sector (convertibility 2015, NEER/REER 2022, RBI forex intervention 2022), and IMF instruments (2022) recur. Pure growth-history and planning questions have thinned but still appear (Hindu rate, plan holidays, IPR 1956, 1991 reforms).
- Statement questions are built from NCERT sentences with one word flipped ("RBI has now adopted WPI as its key measure of inflation" — false, 2020). Expect "appreciation vs depreciation", "increase vs decrease", "revenue vs capital", "current vs capital account", "RBI vs SEBI" swaps.
- Negative marking (−1/3): Economy is a HIGH-ACCURACY block for a well-prepared aspirant because most questions are conceptual and eliminable. Attempt every question where you can confidently falsify one statement in a 2-statement set. Leave questions that hinge on a specific percentage, a launch year of a minor scheme, or a sub-committee name you have never seen. Working target: attempt most of the block, aiming for roughly three-quarters accuracy on attempted questions.

## Chapter map

Time units: "night" = ~2 h weekday session; "Sat block" = ~3 h weekend session. Whole subject budget: ~70 h over 3 weeks (see Revision & test strategy).

## How to teach a topic from this subject

When the learner says "teach me X" / "explain X" (e.g. "repo rate", "fiscal deficit", "REER", "PSL"), respond in this fixed order, keeping the whole thing under ~500 words unless asked for more:

1. **One-line definition** in NCERT/RBI wording, then a one-line "why it exists".
2. **Mechanism and legal home**: who does it (RBI / Govt / SEBI / Parliament), under which Act or Article, and the causal chain (e.g. "repo ↑ → bank cost of funds ↑ → lending rates ↑ → demand ↓ → inflation ↓, with a lag of several quarters"). Use arrows, not paragraphs.
3. **Key facts table** (4–8 rows): structural rule/threshold (as of 2024), year introduced, who sets it, related instruments, what it is NOT. Never state a current numeric policy rate as fact — say "check the latest RBI policy statement" and give the structural facts instead.
4. **What UPSC has asked**: 2–3 actual PYQ themes with year where known (e.g. "2020: expansionist policy — RBI would not raise the MSF rate"; "2022: NEER/REER statements"), and the exact statement that was false and why.
5. **Three traps**: the specific word-swaps or confusions examiners use on this topic (direction, who-issues-whom, revenue vs capital, current vs capital account, regulator mix-ups).
6. **Two statement-style check questions** in UPSC format (2–3 statements + options, or "how many of the above"), then the answer with a one-line justification for EACH statement, not only the correct option.
7. **Link line**: one sentence on how this topic surfaced in the latest Economic Survey/Budget/RBI statement the learner should read (title only), and which NCERT chapter sentence to reread tonight.

Adaptations: for **institutions/organisations**, replace step 2 with "year, HQ, membership, India's status, flagship report, instruments". For **indices**, replace with "publisher, base year, weights, what is excluded, who uses it for what". For **history/planning**, replace step 2 with a 5-line timeline. For **schemes**, give ministry, year, beneficiary criterion, funding pattern, and the one distinguishing feature — and warn that scheme-detail questions are low-yield and should be attempted only when sure.

## Revision & test strategy

- **Study sequence (3 weeks at ~24 h/week: ~10 h on weeknights + ~14 h at weekends)**:

- **Short notes format**: one A4 side per chapter, tables only — no prose. ## Linking current affairs to this subject

**How a news item becomes a Prelims question**: UPSC rarely asks the news itself; it asks the static concept the news sits on. Conversion rule: (1) name the instrument/institution in the news; (2) pull its definition, regulator, Act and mechanism from the static map above; (3) write 2–3 statements where one is the news fact and the others are static facts with one word flipped (who/what/direction/which account); (4) check that each statement is answerable from NCERT/RBI FAQ wording. When reading Survey/Budget/RBI summaries, log only items that have a static anchor; a pure number without a concept is not question material.

Five example conversions (all 2021–2024 events, static-safe):

1. **News (Apr 2022): RBI introduces the Standing Deposit Facility.** → Static anchor: liquidity corridor. Likely question: "Consider: 1. SDF is the floor of the LAF corridor. 2. Under SDF, banks place funds with RBI without receiving collateral. 3. The SDF rate is set above the repo rate." Answer: 1 and 2 correct; 3 false (SDF < repo < MSF). Trap: collateral direction (under reverse repo RBI gives G-secs as collateral; under SDF it does not).

2. **News (Nov–Dec 2022): RBI launches wholesale and retail pilots of e₹ (CBDC).** → Static anchor: money, legal tender, RBI liability. UPSC 2023 asked: "1. It is possible to make payments in a digital currency without using the US dollar or the SWIFT system. 2. A digital currency can be distributed with a condition programmed into it, such as a time-frame for spending it." Both correct. Likely next variants: "e₹ is a liability of RBI" (true); "e₹ is the same as a cryptocurrency" (false — sovereign, legal tender); "e₹ is a payment rail like UPI" (false — UPI moves bank money, e₹ IS money).

3. **News (Mar 2021): Government retains the 4% ±2% inflation target for 2021–26.** → Static anchor: RBI Act amendment 2016, MPC. Question: "1. The target is notified by the Central Government in consultation with RBI once every five years. 2. The MPC has six members, three appointed by the Government. 3. If inflation stays outside the band for two consecutive quarters, RBI must report failure." Answer: 1 and 2 true; 3 false (three consecutive quarters). Trap: "WPI" substituted for "CPI".

4. **News (Sept 2023): JP Morgan announces inclusion of Indian G-secs in its GBI-EM index from June 2024.** → Static anchor: G-sec market, FPI, Fully Accessible Route (2020), capital account. Question: "1. Government securities are regulated by SEBI. 2. Under the Fully Accessible Route, non-residents can invest in specified G-secs without any ceiling. 3. Foreign investment in G-secs is recorded in the current account of the BoP." Answer: only 2 correct (G-secs are under RBI; FPI is in the capital/financial account). Trap: regulator swap and account swap.

5. **News (Budget 2023-24): sharp rise in Union capital expenditure; "effective capital expenditure" reported separately.** → Static anchor: revenue vs capital expenditure, Art 112, ERD. Question: "Which of the following are part of the capital expenditure of the Union Government? 1. Grants-in-aid to States for creation of capital assets 2. Loans to States 3. Interest payments 4. Purchase of defence equipment." Answer: 2 and 4 only (grants to states for capital assets are revenue expenditure of the Centre — hence "effective"; interest is revenue). Trap: the word "capital" in the grant's purpose.

Bonus patterns to recognise: any news on **GST Council decisions** → questions on Art 279A voting (Centre one-third, states two-thirds, three-fourths majority), what is excluded (alcohol vs petroleum), and the SC's 2022 ruling that Council recommendations are not binding; any news on **FATF plenary** → 1989, Paris, grey vs black list, India placed in "regular follow-up" after its 2024 mutual evaluation; any news on **IMF programme for a neighbour** → EFF vs SBA vs RFI/RCF, quota, SDR basket.
