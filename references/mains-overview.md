# UPSC Mains — Overview, Strategy and Qualifying Papers

Read this file when the learner asks about the Mains stage as a whole, the language papers, how Prelims reading carries into Mains, the post-Prelims sprint, notes, or test series. Paper-level content (syllabus decoded, sources by topic, PYQ themes, teaching and grading) lives in `gs1.md`, `gs2.md`, `gs3.md`, `gs4-ethics.md`, `essay.md`, `optional-subjects.md`; the method, templates and evaluation rubric live in `answer-writing.md`. Numbers below are from the CSE Rules as they have stood in recent years (checked against the pattern up to CSE 2024); dates, vacancies and the exact shape of the language papers vary, so say "typically" and point to the current notification at upsc.gov.in. Any data point marked **[update]** must be refreshed from the latest edition of its source before being quoted to a learner.

## Structure and marks

| Paper | Subject | Duration | Marks | Counts toward merit? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper A | One Indian language (candidate's choice from the Eighth Schedule) | 3 h | 300 | No — qualifying, 25% (75/300) |
| Paper B | English | 3 h | 300 | No — qualifying, 25% (75/300) |
| Paper I | Essay | 3 h | 250 | Yes |
| Paper II | General Studies I (Indian heritage and culture, history and geography of the world and society) | 3 h | 250 | Yes |
| Paper III | General Studies II (Governance, Constitution, polity, social justice and international relations) | 3 h | 250 | Yes |
| Paper IV | General Studies III (Technology, economic development, biodiversity, environment, security and disaster management) | 3 h | 250 | Yes |
| Paper V | General Studies IV (Ethics, integrity and aptitude) | 3 h | 250 | Yes |
| Paper VI | Optional subject, Paper 1 | 3 h | 250 | Yes |
| Paper VII | Optional subject, Paper 2 | 3 h | 250 | Yes |

- **Merit total**: 7 merit papers × 250 = **1750** (written) + Personality Test **275** = **2025**. Prelims marks are not carried forward. Papers A and B are of Matriculation/equivalent standard; their marks are never added, but the Essay, GS and optional papers of a candidate who does not reach 25% in *both* A and B are not evaluated at all.
- **Exemption from Paper A** (per the CSE Rules; verify in the year's notification): candidates belonging to **Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Sikkim**, and candidates with benchmark disability in the hearing-impairment sub-category who can show that their board or university exempted them from second- or third-language study. Everyone else, including candidates educated entirely in English, must take Paper A. There is no exemption from Paper B.
- **Medium rules**: Essay, GS I–IV and the optional papers may be written in English or in any one Eighth Schedule language, but the *same* medium must be used for all of them. The exception is a literature optional, which may be answered in the language of that literature regardless of the medium chosen for the rest. Candidates writing in an Indian language may give the English version of technical terms in brackets. Question papers (other than the literature optionals and Papers A/B) are set in Hindi and English only. The optional, the medium and the Paper A language are declared in the application (in recent years through DAF-I, filled after the Prelims result; check the year's notification) and cannot be changed afterwards.
- **Question-paper shape (typical, as in recent years)**: each GS paper has 20 compulsory questions — ten of 10 marks (about 150 words) and ten of 15 marks (about 250 words) — in 180 minutes. Essay: two sections of four topics each, one essay from each section, 125 marks each, 1000–1200 words. GS IV: Section A (concepts, thinkers, quotations and applied questions) and Section B (typically six case studies), roughly half the marks each. Optional papers: eight questions in two sections, five to be attempted, Q1 and Q5 compulsory and at least one more from each section, 50 marks each. Word limits are printed on the paper; answers must be written in the space provided in the Question-cum-Answer (QCA) booklet, no extra sheets are given, and blank portions must be struck off.
- **Schedule (typical)**: five days of examination spread over about ten days, usually in mid/late September: Day 1 (a Friday) Essay alone; Day 2 GS I (forenoon 9:00–12:00) and GS II (afternoon 2:30–5:30); Day 3 GS III and GS IV; then the next weekend Day 4 Paper A and Paper B, Day 5 Optional Papers 1 and 2. Pandemic years shifted this (CSE 2020 Mains were held in January 2021; CSE 2021 Mains in January 2022). Always confirm on upsc.gov.in.
- **Gaps (typical)**: Prelims result about 2–3 weeks after the Prelims (mid-June to early July); Mains roughly 100–110 days after the Prelims; Mains result in December; Personality Tests roughly January–April; final result typically April–May. Plan the Mains sprint from the day after Prelims, not from the result.

## What actually decides a Mains rank

- **Breadth beats depth.** Every GS question is compulsory and the 250 marks are spread over 20 questions. Illustrative arithmetic: a candidate who scores a fair 40% on every question (100/250) outscores one who scores 55% on twelve questions and leaves eight blank (at most about 94/250 even if the twelve include every 15-marker). Cover every syllabus line at "can write 150 words" depth before deepening anything.
- **Speed is a skill, not a trait.** Twenty answers in 180 minutes is 9 minutes per answer on average — about 7 for a 10-marker and 11 for a 15-marker, including reading and underlining the demand. This is built over 8–10 weeks of timed writing; no amount of reading substitutes for it.
- **Structure and presentation are marks.** Introduction that defines or contextualises, body under subheadings that mirror the question's parts, a conclusion that is forward-looking; keywords underlined, a diagram or flowchart where it saves words. Examiners read many copies in a sitting; a scannable answer gets read, a wall of prose gets skimmed.
- **Substantiation is the differentiator.** One datum (Economic Survey, NFHS, NCRB), one committee or report (2nd ARC, Punchhi, Sarkaria, Shanta Kumar), one judgment (Puttaswamy, S.R. Bommai, Kesavananda Bharati), one scheme and one example per answer lift a generic 5 to a specific 8.
- **Keyword coverage of the syllabus.** Questions are set from the syllabus wording ("salient features", "mechanisms, laws, institutions and bodies", "linkages between development and spread of extremism"); answers that use the syllabus's own vocabulary signal to the examiner that the demand was understood.
- **Essay and GS IV are the rank-makers.** In the marks UPSC publishes for recommended candidates each year, Essay and GS IV typically show a wider spread between candidates than GS I–III, which cluster more tightly (verify against the latest published marks **[update]**). These two papers reward practice against a rubric and punish "common sense" attempts.
- **The optional is the largest single swing (500 marks).** Two papers, usually written on the last day, in a subject where the examiner expects depth. A wrong choice, a half-read syllabus, or no test series here costs more than any GS paper can recover. See `optional-subjects.md`.
- **Current affairs feeds every paper, but static anchors score.** Candidates who can tie a news item to the static concept (the Article, the committee, the theory) score; those who only narrate the news do not.
- **Why many clear Prelims and fail Mains**: Prelims rewards recognition, Mains rewards production. The usual causes are no answer writing before the Prelims result, an untouched optional, no full-length tests under time, treating Paper A as a formality, and reading new sources in the last three weeks instead of revising.

## Paper A (Indian language) and Paper B (English)

Both are of Matriculation (Class X) standard, 300 marks, 3 hours, qualifying at 25%. Marks are not counted, but the merit papers of a candidate who fails either are not evaluated. The languages allowed for Paper A are the 22 languages of the Eighth Schedule (Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santhali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu); the script in which each is to be written (e.g., Santhali in Devanagari or Ol Chiki, Sindhi in Devanagari or Arabic) is specified in the notification. The language is declared in the application and cannot be changed later.

**Paper B — English (typical pattern, 300 marks; verify against the latest paper)**

| Question | Task | Marks (typically) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Essay on one of several given topics, about 600 words | 100 |
| 2 | Reading comprehension: a passage followed by five questions | 75 |
| 3 | Précis of a given passage to about one-third of its length, written on the précis sheet provided | 75 |
| 4 | Usage, in several sub-parts: rewrite sentences as directed (voice, speech, tense, degree, sentence type), supply missing words/prepositions, correct forms of verbs, correct faulty sentences, use idioms/phrases in sentences, synonyms/antonyms | 50 |

**Paper A — Indian language (typical pattern, 300 marks; the shape is broadly common across languages but the split varies by language and year)**

| Question | Task | Marks (typically) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Essay on one of several given topics (about 600 words) | 100 |
| 2 | Reading comprehension: passage and short questions | 60 |
| 3 | Précis / summary | 60 |
| 4 | Translation: English passage into the Indian language | 20 |
| 5 | Translation: Indian-language passage into English | 20 |
| 6 | Grammar and basic usage: idioms and proverbs, sentence correction, synonyms/antonyms, one-word substitutions, transformation of sentences | 40 |

Verify the exact split against the previous year's paper on upsc.gov.in (Examinations → Previous Question Papers) before the learner's weekend of practice; UPSC does change weights.

**The common failure.** Candidates who studied in English and chose Paper A in their mother tongue, having never written it formally since school, fail on translation and grammar; Hindi-medium candidates underestimate the précis and usage questions in Paper B. Every cycle some candidates who wrote good GS papers are not evaluated because one of these papers was not opened before the exam; UPSC does not publish how many. The paper is easy for anyone who has practised it once; it is not easy for anyone who has not.

**One weekend of preparation (two 7-hour days, done 4–6 weeks before Mains, repeated for the other paper the next weekend if the learner is unsure of both)**

- Saturday morning (3 h): download the last two years' papers; write the full previous-year paper under time, by hand, in the correct script. Do not skip the translation and précis.
- Saturday afternoon (2 h): self-check against the expected form: essay reached the word count with paragraphs; précis is one-third the length, in the learner's own words, in the third person, with a title; translation reads naturally in the target language.
- Saturday evening (2 h): grammar drill — tense transformation, active/passive, direct/indirect speech, 50 common idioms, 100 synonyms/antonyms; for Paper A, the letter-formation and spelling conventions of the script (matras, conjuncts, sandhi, punctuation).
- Sunday (6–7 h): second paper under time; précis practice on three editorial-length passages; two short essays (400 words) on standard themes (education, environment, women, youth, technology, a social problem); for Paper A, translate two newspaper paragraphs each way.
- Minimum viable target: comfortably above 50% on the self-check; the real bar is 25%, so the aim is certainty, not polish.
- Sources: previous papers at upsc.gov.in (the only source that matters); for English usage, Wren & Martin, *High School English Grammar and Composition* (chapters on Transformation of Sentences, Direct and Indirect Speech, Précis-Writing, Essay-Writing); for Hindi, any standard Class IX–X Hindi *vyakaran* text prescribed by a school board, and Hindi newspaper editorials for précis practice; for other languages, the Class X state-board grammar text and a daily newspaper in that language.
- Tonight, if a learner asks for something small: one précis from today's editorial, and twenty sentences of voice/speech transformation.

**Mains-only topics** (no Prelims yield; start them only when the Prelims is more than 7 months away, otherwise in the sprint):

- **World history** (GS I): 1750s onward — industrial revolution, colonisation, French/American revolutions as ideas, world wars, decolonisation, Cold War, political philosophies. Start: 8–10 months before Mains; 3 weeks of reading, written as 15-marker outlines. UPSC asked whether the American Revolution was an economic revolt against mercantilism (2013), the events leading to the Suez crisis of 1956 (2014), why the industrial revolution first occurred in England (2015), the Western-educated elite in West African anti-colonial struggles (2016); such questions have been fewer in recent years, so keep this topic lean.
- **Post-independence India** (GS I): integration, reorganisation of states, planning, wars, Emergency, 1991. Start with world history; 2 weeks.
- **Indian society** (GS I): salient features, diversity, women's issues, population, poverty, urbanisation, globalisation's effects, social empowerment, communalism, regionalism, secularism. Start 6 months before Mains; NCERT Class XII *Indian Society* and *Social Change and Development in India*, plus NFHS/Census data; 2 weeks.
- **International relations** (GS II): India and its neighbourhood, bilateral/regional/global groupings, effect of developed/developing countries' policies on India, diaspora, international institutions. Start 6 months before Mains, from current affairs with a country-wise notebook; MEA website briefs, ORF and MP-IDSA commentaries; UPSC asked India's role in mitigating the "virus of conflict" in the SCO (2023), among many questions on neighbours and groupings.
- **Internal security** (GS III): extremism and development linkages, external state and non-state actors, communication networks and media, cyber security, money laundering, border security, security forces and mandates. Start 4–5 months before Mains; Ashok Kumar and Vipul Anekant, *Challenges to Internal Security of India* (chapters on left-wing extremism, the North-East, Jammu and Kashmir, cyber security, border management); 2 weeks.
- **Disaster management** (GS III): 1 week, NDMA guidelines and 2nd ARC Report 3.
- **Ethics** (GS IV): the entire paper is Mains-only. Start 5–6 months before Mains; see `gs4-ethics.md`. UPSC asked what "values" and "ethics" mean and why being ethical matters alongside professional competence (2013), whether CSR makes companies more profitable and sustainable (2016), and what a "crisis of conscience" is and how it manifests in the public domain (year to be verified).
- **Essay**: one essay every two weeks from 6 months out, weekly in the sprint; see `essay.md`. Note the shift from issue-based topics (2013–2017, e.g., "Is the colonial mentality hindering India's success?" 2013; "Social media is inherently a selfish medium" 2017) to philosophical/abstract ones from 2018 ("A people that values its privileges above its principles loses both" 2018; "Thinking is like a game, it does not begin unless there is an opposite team" 2023; "The empires of the future will be the empires of the mind" 2024).

## The 100 days after Prelims (Mains sprint)

Assume Prelims in late May or June and Mains in mid/late September: 14–16 weeks. Do not wait for the Prelims result; check the answer key within 48 hours, estimate the score against the previous years' cut-off range (`exam-facts.md`), and start unless the estimate is clearly hopeless.

## Notes and value addition

Mains notes are written for the last three weeks: one page per syllabus topic, readable in five minutes, written during the first reading and only updated afterwards.

**One-page template (per topic, e.g., "Pressure groups", "Inflation targeting", "Urban floods")**

1. Definition in one line, in the syllabus's wording.
2. Constitutional/legal basis or theory (Article, Act, model, thinker).
3. Two or three data points with source and year.
4. One committee or report recommendation.
5. One Supreme Court judgment, if any, with the holding in a phrase.
6. One government scheme or policy and its known weakness.
7. One example or case (Indian, recent, specific) and one international comparison.
8. A diagram or flowchart if the topic lends itself (cycle, hierarchy, cause-effect).
9. The three PYQs on it, as one-line demands.

**Where the data comes from (cite year and source in the answer; refresh every item marked [update] from its latest edition)**

- *Economic Survey* (annual; the "State of the Economy" chapter gives the macro numbers; the social-infrastructure chapter gives health and education spend) **[update annually]**.
- *Union Budget* speech and the "Budget at a Glance" statement (allocations, deficit targets) **[update annually]**.
- *NFHS-5* (2019–21): fertility, anaemia, stunting, institutional births, women's agency indicators **[update when NFHS-6 is released]**.
- *NCRB Crime in India* and *Accidental Deaths and Suicides in India* (annual): crimes against women, cyber crime, farmer suicides, road deaths **[update annually]**.
- *Periodic Labour Force Survey* (NSO; annual report): unemployment and participation rates, female LFPR **[update to the latest round]**.
- *NITI Aayog*: SDG India Index, National Multidimensional Poverty Index (baseline report 2021, progress review 2023), India Innovation Index, Composite Water Management Index **[update to the latest edition]**.
- *Census 2011* (still the reference for population, urbanisation, literacy, sex ratio until a new census is published) **[update when the next census is released]**.
- *2nd ARC* reports (constituted 2005; fifteen reports, 2006–09; the ten most used: 1 Right to Information, 3 Crisis Management, 4 Ethics in Governance, 6 Local Governance, 10 Refurbishing of Personnel Administration, 11 Promoting e-Governance, 12 Citizen Centric Administration, 13 Organisational Structure of Government of India, 14 Strengthening Financial Management Systems, 15 State and District Administration).
- *Supreme Court judgments* (holding in a phrase): Kesavananda Bharati 1973 (basic structure), Indra Sawhney 1992 (50% reservation ceiling, creamy layer), S.R. Bommai 1994 (limits on Article 356), Vishaka 1997 (workplace sexual-harassment guidelines), Lalita Kumari 2013 (FIR mandatory on information disclosing a cognisable offence), NALSA 2014 (recognition of transgender persons as third gender), Shreya Singhal 2015 (Section 66A IT Act struck down), Puttaswamy 2017 (privacy as a fundamental right), Shayara Bano 2017 (instant triple talaq invalid), Navtej Singh Johar 2018 (Section 377 read down), Joseph Shine 2018 (adultery decriminalised), Anoop Baranwal 2023 (Election Commission appointments pending legislation), Supriyo 2023 (same-sex marriage left to the legislature), Association for Democratic Reforms 2024 (electoral bonds scheme struck down), In re Section 6A Citizenship Act 2024 (Section 6A upheld) **[update with later landmark judgments]**.
- International: UNDP *Human Development Report*, World Bank, IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (2021–23), Global Hunger Index (quote with its methodological caveat; the Government of India disputes the method), WHO, ILO **[update to the latest edition]**.

**Examples bank** (kept from day one, see `current-affairs.md`): a running table with columns *example / what it illustrates / papers it fits / source and date*. Target 150–200 entries by Mains, tagged by GS paper and by Essay theme (technology, justice, education, environment, women, governance, federalism). Review it every Sunday; before the exam, reduce it to the 60 that fit the most topics. The same example may serve a GS II answer, a GS IV case study and an Essay paragraph; that reuse is the point.

## Test series and evaluation

- **Why**: the only way to find out whether 20 answers in 180 minutes is possible, which syllabus lines are blank under pressure, and whether the structure survives fatigue in the afternoon paper.
- **Cadence**: sectional tests (8–10 questions, 90 minutes) while a subject is being revised; full-length tests (20 questions, 3 hours, in the afternoon slot at least twice) from 8–10 weeks before Mains; one complete mock exam week on the real timetable about two weeks out; nothing new in the last 10 days. Optional: at least two full-length tests per paper. Essay: at least ten full-length timed essays, each evaluated.
- **Choosing**: any series whose questions follow the UPSC phrasing and whose evaluation returns within a week. Self-evaluation against a model answer is better than slow external evaluation. Free alternative: previous years' papers written under time and graded here.
- **What to look for in an evaluation**: Did the answer address every part of the demand (directive word, each sub-question)? Was the introduction specific? Were subheadings drawn from the question? Was there at least one datum, one example, one authority per answer? Was the conclusion forward-looking and not a repeat of the body? Was the word limit respected? Which answers were skipped or shortened, and why (time or knowledge)? Over a series of tests: is the time-per-answer falling, and are the same syllabus lines blank repeatedly?
- **How Claude evaluates an answer here**: use the *Evaluation rubric* and the feedback format in `answer-writing.md` (demand analysis, structure, content, substantiation, presentation; a score out of the question's marks with a band, what cost marks quoting the learner's own text, and a rewritten weakest section). For essays use *Grading an essay* in `essay.md`; for GS IV use *Grading an ethics answer* in `gs4-ethics.md`; for the optional use *Evaluating optional answers* in `optional-subjects.md`. Never give a score without naming what would raise it by two marks.
- **Using the learner's own scores**: keep a sheet of test, paper, marks, time used, answers left, and the two worst areas. Act on trends, not single tests. A falling time-per-answer with flat marks means content gaps (revise notes); rising marks with answers left blank means speed (half-paper drills); good sectional but poor full-length means stamina (afternoon-slot practice, sleep). Compare only against the learner's own past tests; test-series ranks say little about UPSC marks.

## Common Mains failure patterns

- **Starting answer writing after the Prelims result.** Fix: first answer on day 3 after Prelims; timed answers from week 3 of the sprint.
- **Leaving answers blank in the exam.** Fix: a 20-answer paper is a budget; cap every answer at its time; write a structured four-point answer for anything unknown rather than nothing; the QCA booklet has a fixed space for each question, so answers may be attempted in order of confidence, but no space is to be left unfilled.
- **Reading new sources in the last three weeks.** Fix: a frozen source list by week 10; after that, notes only. Anything new goes on a single "last-minute" page.
- **An optional that was never fully read or never tested.** Fix: optional first in the sprint if it is untouched; two full-length tests per paper; if the gap is unbridgeable in the time left, say so and move to damage limitation (compulsory questions and the most-asked sections).
- **Treating Paper A or Paper B as a formality.** Fix: the one-weekend plan above, done 4–6 weeks out, both papers if the learner has not written the language since school.
- **Generic answers with no data, example or authority.** Fix: the one-page notes template and the examples bank; the rule "no answer leaves the pen without one number, one name and one case".
- **Essay and GS IV left to "common sense".** Fix: ten evaluated essays and twenty evaluated case studies before Mains; learn the rubric, not just the topic.
- **Ignoring the syllabus wording and PYQ patterns.** Fix: write the 150-word answer frame for every syllabus line; outline the last ten years' questions on each line before reading it.
- **Poor presentation under fatigue.** Fix: subheadings, underlining, one diagram per paper in every full-length test; afternoon-slot practice; the mock exam week.
- **A plan that ignores the person's hours.** Fix: for a working professional, arrange 4–6 weeks of leave before Mains, use the weeknight fallback rule, keep Sunday evenings free, and size every "tonight" task to the two hours actually available.
