# Environment & Ecology — Resource Map

## Weight and exam behaviour
- **Weight:** roughly 10–20 questions out of 100 in GS Paper I (Prelims) in recent years; the exact count varies with how environment-flavoured Geography/S&T questions are classified, so do not plan around a fixed number. Since the Prelims became common with the Indian Forest Service exam (2013 onward), Environment has stayed one of the heaviest sections alongside Polity and Economy. Treat it as a scoring, not optional, section.
- **Formats used:** (a) multi-statement questions ("which of the statements given above is/are correct" and, from 2022–23, "how many of the above statements are correct"); (b) pair-matching (wetland–state, species–habitat, protected area–river basin); (c) "which one of the following best describes the term X" for new vocabulary (blue carbon, carbon fertilisation, biochar, net metering, microbeads); (d) direct species/status identification ("which of the above is/are critically endangered"); (e) convention/organisation statements (year, secretariat, parent body, protocol).
- **Trend, 2011 to 2024:** 2011–2014 drew heavily on Shankar's text (ecology basics, pollutants, schedules). 2015–2018 shifted toward conventions, climate-finance terms, and species-location matching. 2019–2024 is a hybrid: static concepts (NCERT ecology, nutrient cycles, pyramids) + current-affairs-hooked terms (agri-biotech, waste rules, Mission LiFE, Kunming-Montreal, carbon markets) + protected-area geography. The "how many statements" format removed the old two-option elimination shortcut.
- **Negative marking (one-third of the marks per wrong answer):** attempt when you can eliminate two of four options, or when you are sure about one statement that fixes the answer. In "how many statements" format, attempt only if you know all but one statement. Species-status questions are the highest-risk area — do not guess IUCN categories.
- **Where marks are lost:** confusing endangered vs critically endangered, wrong state for a protected area, mixing UNFCCC instruments with CBD protocols, and misreading "which is NOT" questions. Each of these is fixable by tables, not by more reading.

## Chapter map

Reading order: NCERT ecology unit first, then Shankar units in the sequence below (editions reorder units and chapter numbers; follow chapter *titles* and this order regardless). Time budget: **48 hours total = 2 weeks** at ~10 weekday hours + ~14 weekend hours. The pollution rows are sub-sections of Shankar's single chapter **"Environmental Pollution"**, not separate chapters.

## How to teach a topic from this subject

When the learner says "teach me X" / "explain X" (X may be a concept, species, protected area, act, convention, pollutant or scheme), answer in this fixed order, under 400 words unless asked for more:

1. **One-line definition** in UPSC-usable wording (the phrasing the exam would paraphrase).
2. **Basis:** scientific mechanism (for ecology/pollution/climate topics) *and/or* legal-institutional basis (which Act/Rule/Convention, which body declares or administers, which year).
3. **Key facts table** (5–8 rows): year | place/state | number | status (IUCN / WPA schedule / CITES appendix / Ramsar / BR) | nodal body or publisher | parent treaty or Act. Leave a cell blank rather than guess; say "verify on IUCN/NTCA" when a figure changes yearly.
4. **What UPSC has asked:** 2–4 bullets with year and the exact angle (e.g. "2020 — which protected areas lie in the Cauvery basin"; "2016 — were the SDGs proposed by the Club of Rome"). If no direct PYQ, say so and name the nearest sibling topic that was asked.
5. **Three traps:** the confusions the options will exploit (status mix-ups, parent-treaty mix-ups, state mix-ups, "not" questions).
6. **Two statement-style check questions** in UPSC format (two or three statements; "which is/are correct" or "how many are correct"), with answer and one-line justification for each statement.
7. **Tonight's action:** the exact chapter title and sub-heading in Shankar/NCERT to read (10–20 minutes), and the row to add to which personal table.

Adaptations: for a *species*, step 2 is "habitat + threats" and the table must include IUCN, WPA schedule, CITES, one anchor PA. For a *protected area*, table must include state, river/landscape, flagship species, special legal tags (TR/BR/Ramsar/WHS). For a *convention*, table must include adopted/in-force years, secretariat, parent, protocols under it, India's status. For a *pollutant*, table must include source, effect/disease, standard or rule, control technology. Never introduce post-2024 current affairs unless the learner asks about the news itself.

## Revision & test strategy

- **Three revisions, fixed timing.** R1: next evening, 20 minutes re-reading only your highlights and the "Make-a-note" table for the chapter. R2: the weekend after finishing a unit, from your short notes only (not the book). R3: in the final 45 days before Prelims, tables only — species, PAs by state, acts, conventions, reports/publishers, pollutant–disease — one table per night, two cycles.
- **Short notes format:** no prose. Six master tables (listed above) plus one page per Shankar unit of "trap pairs" (A vs B, with the discriminating fact). Target: the entire subject in 25–30 A4 pages or one spreadsheet with filter columns (state, status, year). Update the species and PA tables when a news item appears; never start a new notebook for current affairs.
- **PYQs:** do all Environment questions 2011–2024 (and the Environment-flavoured Geography/S&T ones). Sequence: topic-wise immediately after each unit (ecology after NCERT + Shankar Ecology; species/PAs after Biodiversity; conventions after Acts/Conventions); then year-wise full sets of 2018–2024 in the last 60 days to calibrate the "how many statements" format. Keep a wrong-answer log with the trap type (status / state / parent treaty / "not" misread).
- **Sectional tests:** Test 1 at the end of week 1 (Ecology + Biodiversity + Protected Areas); Test 2 at the end of week 2 (full Environment). Then stop sectional tests and rely on full-length GS tests, reviewing only the Environment questions from each. Target in sectional tests: about 70% correct on attempted, attempting about 70% of questions.
- **Attempt rule for the exam:** aim to attempt roughly two-thirds of the Environment questions on the paper with accuracy above 75%. Skip species-status questions unless at least two of the three listed species are certain.

## Linking current affairs to this subject

**Conversion formula.** For any environment news item, extract six hooks and the question writes itself: (1) *Name* → what is it (species, site, scheme, treaty, term); (2) *Locate* → state / river / biogeographic zone / country; (3) *Status* → IUCN, WPA schedule, CITES appendix, Ramsar/BR/TR/WHS tag; (4) *Institution* → who declared, who publishes, under which Act or convention; (5) *Mechanism* → what it actually does or requires; (6) *Number* → year, count, target. UPSC then tests the static hooks (2–5), not the news itself; so add the item as a row in the relevant master table and read the Shankar page that covers its parent concept.

**Example conversions (static-safe, all pre-2025):**

1. **News (2022–23): Cheetahs translocated to Kuno National Park.** → Static hooks: Kuno is in Madhya Pradesh (Sheopur district), Kuno river is a Chambal tributary; cheetah declared extinct in India in 1952; sourced from Namibia (2022) and South Africa (2023); cheetah IUCN Vulnerable, Asiatic cheetah subspecies Critically Endangered (Iran). → Likely question: three statements on state, source country and extinction year; trap: Kuno vs Gandhi Sagar, cheetah vs leopard status.
2. **News (2022): Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act 2022 passed.** → Hooks: schedules reduced from six to four; CITES implementation inserted; vermin schedule removed; Centre empowered to regulate invasive alien species; captive-elephant transfer exception. → Question: "how many of the above statements are correct" on schedule count and CITES; trap: old six-schedule structure from older books.
3. **News (2022): COP27 Sharm el-Sheikh agrees to a Loss and Damage Fund.** → Hooks: under the UNFCCC process; loss-and-damage mechanism first set up at Warsaw 2013 (WIM); GCF (Cancun 2010) is a different fund; Egypt hosted. → Question: pair COP–outcome (Cancun–GCF, Warsaw–REDD+/WIM, Paris–NDCs, Glasgow–coal phase-down, Sharm el-Sheikh–L&D fund); trap: attributing GCF to Paris.
4. **News (2022–24): New Ramsar sites declared in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Odisha, Bihar, MP etc.** → Hooks: Ramsar 1971, Iran; secretariat Gland (IUCN-hosted); designation does not by itself create a legal protected area under WPA; Montreux Record has only Keoladeo and Loktak; Tamil Nadu has the most sites. → Question: wetland–state pairs plus one statement on Montreux; trap: assuming all Ramsar sites are sanctuaries, or that Chilika is still on Montreux.
5. **News (2022): Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework adopted at CBD COP15.** → Hooks: under CBD (Rio 1992; secretariat Montreal); replaces Aichi Targets (Nagoya 2010); 30×30 target (30% land and sea protected by 2030); Nagoya Protocol is on access and benefit sharing; Cartagena on LMOs. → Question: statements on which protocol does what and what GBF replaced; trap: mixing CBD protocols with UNFCCC instruments.
6. (Spare) **News (2023): Supreme Court modifies its 2022 one-kilometre eco-sensitive-zone order around protected areas.** → Hooks: ESZs notified under EPA 1986 (not WPA); NBWL's 2002 guideline suggested 10 km; ESZ ≠ ESA (Western Ghats, Kasturirangan); buffer vs ESZ in tiger reserves. → Question: "ESZs are declared under which Act" with WPA as the distractor.
