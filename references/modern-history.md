# Modern Indian History — Resource Map

## Weight and exam behaviour
- Typical load: a handful of standalone Modern History questions in GS Paper I Prelims each year (roughly 4-8; count them yourself from the papers you compile — the number varies by year), and more if the "freedom-struggle personalities / press / culture" overlap is counted. It is the most reliable history block to score from because the syllabus is finite and the primary book is one volume.
- Formats UPSC uses here: (1) "Consider the following statements" (2-3 statements, pick correct ones); (2) matched pairs — organisation/founder, movement/leader, newspaper/editor, term/meaning (e.g. Aurang, Banian, Mirasidar in 2020); (3) chronological ordering (e.g. sequencing Second RTC / Quit India / RIN Mutiny); (4) biographical riddle ("He wrote biographies of Mazzini, Garibaldi, Shivaji and Shrikrishna; stayed in America..." = Lala Lajpat Rai, 2018); (5) since 2023, Statement-I / Statement-II assertion-reason pairs.
- Trend (2013-2024, qualitative): 2013-2017 were heavier and mostly mainstream (Acts, INC sessions, Gandhian movements, 1919 Act features in 2017). 2017-2020 shifted toward organisations, press, labour/peasant movements and early-colonial economy and institutions (Trade Disputes Act 1929 in 2017, Charter Act 1813 in 2018 and 2019, Fort William College in 2020). 2021-2024 ask fewer but more granular questions — early English factories (2021), exact provisions of missions (Cripps 2022), personalities' lesser-known associations (Bethune School 2021), colonial archaeologists (2023). The depth required has moved from "chapter heading" to "sub-paragraph of Spectrum".
- Negative marking: one-third of a question's marks per wrong answer. Attempt a statements question only when you can eliminate with a date/sequence anchor; in pair-matching attempt only if you are sure of all-but-one pair; skip pure riddle questions where the clue set is unfamiliar. Aim for a hit rate of 75%+ on attempted modern-history questions; this block should not cost you marks.
- Repetition is real: Charter Acts, 1919/1935 Act provisions, Swadeshi-era institutions, Butler Committee, Ryotwari and Permanent Settlement, Gandhi's three early satyagrahas, Cripps, INA and Quit India have each appeared more than once since 2013.

Time budget: the whole first pass, including PYQs for each chapter, must fit the 2.5 weeks (~60 h) the master plan allots: Week 1 (~24 h) = rows from "Sources" to "Indian National Congress: Foundation and the Moderate Phase"; Week 2 (~24 h) = "Era of Militant Nationalism" to "Independence with Partition"; final half-week (~12 h) = "Constitutional, Administrative and Judicial Developments" to "The Movement of the Working Class" plus the Governors-General/Viceroys appendix. ## How to teach a topic from this subject

When the learner says "teach me X" or "explain X" (X = an event, Act, movement, organisation or person):

1. Place it (2 lines): date range, Governor-General/Viceroy in office, and what came immediately before and after it. Modern history questions are mostly sequence questions in disguise.
2. Cause → event → outcome (5-8 lines): why it happened, what exactly happened (places, leaders, dates), and what it led to (Act, pact, split, next movement).
3. Key-facts table (5-10 rows): leader(s) / place / date / organisation / document or slogan / British counterpart / outcome. For an Act: provisions as "first-time" bullets. For a movement: methods, spread, social groups involved, why withdrawn.
4. What UPSC has asked (2-4 lines): cite the PYQ years and the angle (pair-matching, chronology, statement) only when you are sure of the year; otherwise say "has been asked". Reproduce one real PYQ if it exists.
5. Three traps: the near-identical names, dates or places that the examiner swaps (e.g. 1919 Hunter vs 1882 Hunter; Bardoli 1922 resolution vs Bardoli 1928 satyagraha; August Declaration vs August Offer).
6. Two statement-style check questions, each with 2-3 statements and the answer code, written in UPSC's phrasing ("Consider the following statements... Which of the statements given above is/are correct?"). One should test the sequence, one the attribution.
7. Close with a "note this" line: which row(s) of which table the learner should add tonight, and the Spectrum chapter title to open for more.

Keep the whole teaching reply under ~500 words unless the learner asks for depth; the goal is one-night digestibility, not a Mains essay. For a person ("teach me about Aurobindo"), replace step 2 with a 6-line timeline of their life and step 3 with a works/organisations/papers table. For a comparison ("NCM vs CDM"), use a two-column table in step 3.

## Revision & test strategy

## Linking current affairs to this subject

How it works: a news item in this subject is an anniversary, a commemoration day, a renamed place or award, a new statue/museum/UNESCO tag, or a personality in a government scheme name. UPSC does not ask the news; it asks the static fact the news points at — the person's organisation, journal, movement, year, or the Act/event of that date. Procedure: (1) identify the static anchor (person, event, place, Act); (2) open that Spectrum sub-section; (3) write the 5-7 facts the examiner could turn into statements; (4) write one pair-matching and one statement question yourself; (5) add the row to the relevant table.

Example conversions (all pre-2025):

1. News (Nov 2021): 15 November declared Janjatiya Gaurav Divas on Birsa Munda's birth anniversary. Static: Munda Ulgulan 1899-1900, Chhotanagpur, khuntkatti land rights, "Dharti Aba", Birsa died in Ranchi jail 1900; Chhotanagpur Tenancy Act 1908. Already asked: "Ulgulan or the Great Tumult" (2020). Likely question: pair tribal revolts (Santhal, Munda, Kol, Khond) with region/leader.
2. News (Aug 2021): 14 August declared Partition Horrors Remembrance Day. Static: Mountbatten Plan 3 June 1947; Indian Independence Act 18 July 1947; Radcliffe Boundary Commissions; referenda in NWFP and Sylhet; Punjab/Bengal assembly votes; Gandhi in Noakhali/Calcutta. Likely question: statements on the provisions of the 3 June Plan vs the Independence Act.
3. News (Jan 2021 and Sept 2022): 23 January declared Parakram Diwas; Netaji statue unveiled at India Gate. Static: Bose's escape 1941, Azad Hind Government 21 Oct 1943 (Singapore), Andaman renamed Shaheed/Swaraj Dec 1943, Rani Jhansi Regiment, Moirang 1944, Red Fort trials Nov 1945 (Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal, G.S. Dhillon), Forward Bloc 1939, Haripura/Tripuri. Already asked: 8 Aug 1942 (2021). Likely question: chronology of INA events or Bose's INC presidencies.
4. News (2024): Centenary of the Vaikom Satyagraha (1924-25). Static: Travancore, temple-road access, T.K. Madhavan, K.P. Kesava Menon, Periyar ("Vaikom Veerar"), Gandhi's visit 1925, Narayana Guru and SNDP 1903, Guruvayur satyagraha 1931-32 (K. Kelappan), Temple Entry Proclamation 1936 (Chithira Thirunal). Already asked: Self-Respect Movement pair (2019). Likely question: pairs of anti-caste movements with leaders/regions.
5. News (Sept 2023): Santiniketan inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Static: Tagore — Visva-Bharati 1921, Nobel 1913 (*Gitanjali*), knighthood renounced 1919 after Jallianwala, Raksha Bandhan during Swadeshi 1905, "Amar Sonar Bangla", "Jana Gana Mana" first sung Calcutta 1911, Tagore-Gandhi debate on the charkha. Likely question: statements on Tagore's responses to Swadeshi and Jallianwala.

Other anchors from the same period worth one table-row each: Jallianwala Bagh centenary (2019) → Hunter Committee, Dyer, Rowlatt Act; Chauri Chaura centenary (Feb 2021) → withdrawal of NCM, Bardoli resolution; Sri Aurobindo's 150th birth anniversary (Aug 2022) → Alipore bomb case, *Bande Mataram*, *Karmayogin*, Bengal National College; 75 years of Independence / "Azadi ka Amrit Mahotsav" (2021-23) → lesser-known freedom fighters (Alluri Sitarama Raju's Rampa rebellion 1922-24, Tirot Singh, Rani Gaidinliu, Kanaklata Barua, Matangini Hazra); Mahatma Gandhi's 150th (2019) → South Africa phase, Champaran, Kheda, Ahmedabad.
