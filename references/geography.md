# Geography — Resource Map

## Weight and exam behaviour
- Typical load: roughly 10–18 questions a year when you count core physical/Indian geography, mapping and "place in news", and the geography half of environment/agriculture/resources questions. The exact count depends on how borderline questions are classified; treat it as approximate. It is one of the largest static blocks and the most map-dependent.
- Formats UPSC uses here: (a) "Consider the following statements… how many of the above are correct" (dominant since 2022); (b) Statement-I/Statement-II causal pairs (from 2023; e.g. atmospheric heating by terrestrial vs solar radiation, 2023); (c) pair-matching of place ↔ state/range/river/country (peaks–ranges 2022, glaciers–rivers 2019, wetlands–states 2022, reservoirs–states 2022, conflict regions–countries 2023); (d) ordering/sequence questions (e.g. Himalayan tributaries of the Ganga west→east; states by forest cover); (e) single-answer "why" explanations (equatorial counter-current 2015, annual range of temperature in continental interiors 2013, longest day 2022); (f) route/count questions (Kohima→Kottayam minimum number of states).
- Trend 2017–2024 (qualitative): 2017–2019 were map-heavy (Indian rivers, glaciers, seas, hills, places in news). 2020–2022 mixed physical concepts (jet streams and cyclone eye 2020, cloud radiative effect 2022) with mapping (peaks, wetlands, reservoirs 2022). 2023–2024 leaned on Class-11 physical geography concepts (P/S waves and atmospheric heating 2023, products of volcanic eruptions 2024) asked as multi-statement counts, plus a few country-list questions (North Sea littorals 2024). Human geography from Class 12 is near-absent; Indian economic geography appears mainly through agriculture/resources (e.g. India vs China arable and irrigated area, 2024).
- Negative marking: physical-geography concept questions reward attempts if you can eliminate one statement with certainty (in the "how many" format one confirmed-false statement usually kills two options). Pure matching questions on obscure places (reservoirs, wetlands) should be attempted only if you can confirm two of the pairs; otherwise skip. Never attempt a sequence question from a vague mental map.
- Mapping questions look hard but are low-variance for a prepared candidate: the same few hundred locations recycle (see "Mapping / data to memorise"). This is where a working professional gets the cheapest marks per hour.

## Chapter map

Reading order: Fundamentals of Physical Geography (FPG) → India: Physical Environment (IPE) → Leong (selected) → Class 12 selective → atlas drills throughout. "1 night" = ~2 h weeknight; "Sat 3h" = weekend block. The whole block fits 3 weeks at ~24 h/week: **Week 1 = FPG**, **Week 2 = IPE + atlas**, **Week 3 = Leong selected + Class 12 selective + world mapping**. Where two chapters share a night, they are marked "½ night".

- Earth: oceanic crust ~5 km, continental ~30 km (up to ~70 km under mountains); Moho; mantle down to 2,900 km (Gutenberg discontinuity); outer core liquid; inner core solid from ~5,150 km (Lehmann discontinuity); radius ~6,370 km.
- Oceans: average salinity 35‰; ~97% of Earth's water is saline; of fresh water ~70% is in ice sheets and glaciers, most of the rest groundwater; shelf break at roughly 200 m (Leong), NCERT: average shelf width ~80 km, gradient about 1° or less; deepest point Challenger Deep, Mariana Trench.
- Heat budget (NCERT): 100 units in → 35 reflected (albedo: 27 by clouds, 2 by snow and ice, 6 scattered), 14 absorbed by the atmosphere, 51 by the surface; perihelion 3 January, aphelion 4 July; solstices 21 June / 22 December; equinoxes 21 March / 23 September.
- India: 8°4'–37°6'N, 68°7'–97°25'E; IST 82°30'E; coastline ~7,500 km; 3.28 million km² (~2.4% of world land area); 9 coastal states, 4 coastal UTs (DNH and DD, Puducherry, Lakshadweep, A&N); Tropic of Cancer through 8 states; 82°30'E through 5 states.

## How to teach a topic from this subject

When the learner says "teach me X" (e.g. "teach me the monsoon mechanism", "explain karst"), follow this order, in under ~600 words, then stop and ask whether to go deeper:

1. **One-line definition** in plain words, plus the geographic scale (local/regional/global) and the NCERT chapter it lives in (name the chapter so they can open it tonight).
2. **Mechanism / scientific basis**: cause → process → result, 4–6 steps max. Always state the controlling variables (temperature, pressure, Coriolis, rock type, slope, moisture). For Indian topics, add the regional driver (Himalaya, Tibetan plateau, peninsular structure).
3. **Map anchor**: where it is on the atlas (latitude band, ocean, state, coast). Tell the learner to physically find it on the atlas page before continuing — geography questions are lost on location, not theory.
4. **Key facts table**: 5–8 rows, columns chosen for the topic (type / cause / example / location; or season / winds / rain where). Use only textbook values.
5. **What UPSC has asked**: 2–4 PYQ themes with year where known (e.g. "jet streams occur only in the NH — 2020, false"), phrased as the statement UPSC tested and whether it was true or false. Give a year only when certain; otherwise say "asked earlier".
6. **Three traps**: the specific confusions UPSC exploits (direction reversed, hemisphere flipped, cause ↔ effect swapped, nearby look-alike names). Write each as "Trap: ... Correct: ...".
7. **Two statement-style check questions** in current UPSC format ("Consider the following statements... How many of the above are correct?" or Statement-I/Statement-II). Give answers only after the learner replies; explain each statement, including why wrong ones are wrong.
8. **Note instruction**: tell the learner exactly which table/list from the Chapter map to add to their short notes for this topic, and which atlas page to mark.

Adaptations: for a landform, replace step 2 with agent → erosional vs depositional → stage; for a river, replace with source → course → tributaries (L/R) → mouth → dams; for a climate type, use location → temperature → rainfall regime → vegetation → crops/activities; for a mapping-only item (strait, lake), compress to steps 1, 3, 4, 6, 7.

## Revision & test strategy
- **One 3-week block, then three revision passes.** Pass 1 is the 3-week block above (~24 h/week): Week 1 FPG, Week 2 IPE with the atlas, Week 3 Leong selected + Class 12 selective + world mapping, making only the tables listed in the Chapter map. Pass 2 (about 1 week, slotted into the general revision cycle after all subjects are done once): redo every table from memory, finish the mapping lists, solve PYQs 2017–2024. Pass 3 (3–4 days, 2–3 months before the exam): tables and atlas only, no textbooks. A final 2-day flip of tables in the last week.
- **Short notes = tables and blank maps, not prose.** Target ~25–30 pages total: physiography table, drainage table, soil table, vegetation table, climate-season table, monsoon flow diagram, cyclone comparison, Koeppen/Leong climate table, currents list, local winds list, mineral-and-coal map list, ports list, world straits/seas/conflict-region list. Keep a separate "Traps" page where each confusion is one line ("Namcha Barwa — Arunachal, NOT Garhwal").
- **Atlas drill**: 15 minutes, three nights a week, from a blank outline map: trace rivers one week, ranges the next, then world straits/seas, then conflict regions. Mapping marks come from this habit, not from reading.
- **PYQs**: solve every geography question from 2011–2024. Do 2011–2016 at the end of the 3-week block (they test concepts cleanly), 2017–2024 in Pass 2 (they test mapping and multi-statement counting). For each wrong answer, write the trap in one line on the Traps page. Re-solve 2019–2024 in Pass 3 at about 1.2 min/question.
- **Sectional tests**: one after the Physical Geography block (FPG + Leong), one after the Indian Geography block (IPE + Class 12 selective), one mapping-only test (30 Qs on places), then geography-only full tests fortnightly from Pass 2. Analyse by error type: concept (reread chapter), location (atlas drill), elimination failure (practise statement-count logic), guessing (tighten attempt rule).
- **Attempt rule for the actual paper**: attempt a physical-geography concept Q when you can confirm at least one statement as false; attempt a matching Q only when two pairs are certain; skip sequence questions unless you can draw the map in your head; expect to leave 2–3 geography questions unattempted.

## Linking current affairs to this subject
A geography news item is never asked as news. UPSC strips the event and tests the static fact underneath: the location (map), the mechanism (NCERT process), or the classification (type/category). Conversion method: (1) locate it — country/state, river, range, sea; (2) name the NCERT concept it demonstrates; (3) list 3 nearby look-alikes UPSC could swap in; (4) write one statement that is true and one that is subtly false; (5) file it in the static table, not in a current-affairs note.

Five example conversions (all pre-2025 events):

1. **Turkey–Syria earthquake (February 2023)** → static Q on seismic waves and plate setting: "In a seismograph, P waves are recorded earlier than S waves" (asked 2023, true); "S waves pass through the outer core" (false); the East Anatolian Fault is a strike-slip (transform) boundary between the Anatolian and Arabian plates; Himalayan earthquakes come from continent–continent convergence, with no volcanism.
2. **Hunga Tonga–Hunga Ha'apai eruption (January 2022)** → "Products of volcanic eruptions include pyroclastic debris, ash, nitrogen compounds and sulphur compounds" (asked 2024; NCERT lists all of these); location in the South Pacific on the Ring of Fire (Tonga trench, Pacific plate subducting beneath the Australian plate); submarine eruption generating a tsunami; Barren Island as India's only active volcano; shield vs composite volcano.
3. **Joshimath land subsidence (January 2023)** → Uttarakhand, Chamoli district, near Vishnuprayag (Alaknanda–Dhauliganga confluence) and the Main Central Thrust; mass-movement types (creep, subsidence, slump) from "Geomorphic Processes"; Alaknanda's five prayags; glacier sources (Satopanth → Alaknanda) — exactly the 2019 glacier–river pair format.
4. **Cyclone Biparjoy (Arabian Sea, June 2023) / Cyclone Mocha (Bay of Bengal, May 2023)** → why the Bay of Bengal produces more cyclones than the Arabian Sea; cyclone naming by RSMC New Delhi for 13 panel countries; "the eye is the warmest, calmest part; only some cyclones develop an eye" (2020); pre-monsoon (May–June) and post-monsoon (Oct–Dec) seasons; landfall areas (Biparjoy: Kachchh, Gujarat; Mocha: Rakhine coast, Myanmar, near the Bangladesh border); cyclones do not form within ~5° of the equator (Coriolis).
5. **Bidibidi refugee settlement / Tigray and Sudan conflicts (2022–2023)** → place ↔ country pairs: Bidibidi is in north-western Uganda, hosting South Sudanese refugees (asked 2022); Tigray is in Ethiopia, Kachin in Myanmar, Donbas in Ukraine (asked 2023, all three draft pairs were wrong); Darfur in Sudan; convert any such news into a "region — country" line in the conflict-region list, and add its neighbours (e.g. Uganda borders South Sudan, DRC, Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda).

Other reliable conversions of the same kind: river-linking approvals (Ken–Betwa, cleared 2021 → Yamuna's tributaries, MP origin, Panna) and interstate water disputes (Cauvery, Krishna, Mahadayi → tributaries, basin states, dams); IMD El Niño/IOD declarations (2023) → Walker circulation, Peru current, monsoon break; heatwaves (2022–2024) → Loo, inversion, urban heat island; ISFR release → forest-type definitions and state ranks; new Ramsar sites → lake origin/state pairs (the 2022 wetland Q format); new national waterway or port news → the major-ports list and NW-1 to NW-5.
