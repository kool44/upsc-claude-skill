# Indian Polity & Governance — Resource Map

## Weight and exam behaviour
- Typically 12-18 questions out of 100 in GS Paper I Prelims (varies by year); among the heaviest static subjects and the most "closable" one (finite syllabus, one book).
- Formats: (a) "Which of the statements given above is/are correct?" with 2-4 statements (dominant); (b) "Consider the following" list-selection (e.g., which bodies are constitutional); (c) match-the-following (articles/schedules/committees); (d) occasional direct one-liners on a constitutional provision.
- Trend 2017-2024 (qualitative): shift from fact recall ("which article") to conceptual/applied questions ("what is the effect of X", "in the context of Y, which statement is correct"); more questions on Parliament procedure, judiciary, elections/ECI, federalism and governance bodies in the news; 2021-2024 papers use "how many of the above are correct" counting, which punishes partial knowledge.
- Negative marking (1/3 mark per wrong answer): attempt Polity questions where you can eliminate at least 2 options; one strong fact (an article number, a "with/without reasons" phrase, a "simple/special majority" distinction) usually eliminates two options. Avoid guessing on "how many of the above" questions unless sure of every statement.
- Laxmikanth coverage is close to complete for static questions; misses are usually from Parliament rules of procedure, recent Supreme Court judgments, or bills (PRS).

## Chapter map

Primary source: M. Laxmikanth, *Indian Polity* (chapter titles as in the current 6th/7th editions; where your edition differs, go by title; the book's Part II "System of Government" holds Parliamentary System, Federal System, Centre-State Relations, Inter-State Relations and Emergency Provisions; Part III "Central Government" starts with President). Time budget is for a working professional: ~10 weekday hours + ~14 weekend hours = ~24 hours/week, so the whole first reading fits **3 weeks (~70 hours)**. Times below are reading time per chapter; do the chapter's PYQs in the same sitting (10-15 min). Suggested split: Week 1 = Historical Background to Emergency Provisions (~22h); Week 2 = President to Scheduled and Tribal Areas (~26h); Week 3 = Election Commission to the end plus the three appendices (~22h). Chapters marked Skip are not read on the first pass; read them only after PYQs 2011-2024 are done.

## How to teach a topic from this subject

When the learner says "teach me X" or "explain X" in Polity, follow this order and keep it to one screen:

1. **Definition in one line** - plain words first, then the constitutional phrase.
2. **Constitutional/legal basis** - the article(s) or Part, the enabling Act (if any), the amendment that introduced or changed it, and the landmark case that fixed its meaning. Quote key phrases verbatim where UPSC quotes them ("armed rebellion", "proved misbehaviour or incapacity", "as soon as possible").
3. **Key facts table** - 5-10 rows: who appoints / who removes / tenure / majority required / which House / time limits / binding or advisory. Where a parallel body exists (President-Governor, SC-HC, UPSC-SPSC, CIC-SIC, RS-Legislative Council, 73rd-74th), teach as a two-column comparison.
4. **What UPSC has asked** - cite 2-4 actual PYQ themes from the compiled 2011-2024 set (e.g., money bill vs financial bill; powers of the Governor under Art. 200/201; Lok Adalat features; "how many of the above" sets on the Speaker or on constitutional bodies). Give the year only when it is verified from the PYQ booklet; otherwise say "asked more than once since 2016". Name the statement that was the trap.
5. **Three traps** - the confusions listed in the Chapter map for that chapter, written as "X is NOT Y" statements.
6. **Two statement-style check questions** - in UPSC format ("Consider the following statements: 1. ... 2. ... Which of the statements given above is/are correct?") and one "how many of the above" variant; give the answer with a one-line reason for each statement only after the learner attempts.
7. **Tonight's action** - point to the exact Laxmikanth chapter title and the table to make; say what to skip within it.

Adaptations: for a case (e.g., Kesavananda), replace step 3 with a timeline (prior cases -> amendment -> holding -> later cases). For a body (e.g., CVC), step 3 is the appointment/removal/term table plus "constitutional/statutory/executive" status. For a procedure (e.g., money bill), step 3 is a flow (origin -> certification -> other House -> President) with time limits. For a current-affairs item, start from the news in one line, then run steps 2-6 on the static provision behind it.

## Revision & test strategy

- **Readings:** 1 full reading of Laxmikanth (3 weeks on the schedule above, ~70 hours), then 3 revisions: R1 within 2 weeks of finishing (from the book, ~12 hours over 1 week), R2 from own short notes only (~5 hours), R3 in the last 30 days before Prelims (~3 hours, notes + flagged PYQs). Revise Fundamental Rights, Parliament, President/Governor, judiciary, amendments and local government a fourth time in the final week.
- **Short notes format:** no prose. Only (a) comparison tables (President vs Governor; SC vs HC; RS vs Legislative Council; 73rd vs 74th; 323A vs 323B; constitutional vs statutory bodies; NCSC/NCST/NCBC), (b) number sheet (majorities, time limits, ages, strengths), (c) article flashcards (the list above), (d) one page of "traps" copied from the Chapter map and from every PYQ you got wrong, (e) amendments list. Target 25-30 pages total; cap at one page per chapter.
- **PYQs:** do all Polity questions 2011-2024 (roughly 200). First pass chapter-wise right after each chapter; second pass as a full-subject timed set after R1; third pass only the wrong-marked ones before R3. Give extra weight to 2017-2024 because the statement style matches the current paper. Pre-2011 questions are fact-heavy and lower yield; skip unless time allows.
- **Sectional tests:** 2 Polity sectional tests (100 Qs each) after R1, 1 after R2; analyse every wrong answer back to the exact chapter line and add it to the traps page. Do not take Polity sectionals before finishing the first reading. From 60 days before Prelims, only full-length tests; from each, extract Polity errors into the traps page.
- **Working targets in sectionals (not cutoffs):** 75%+ accuracy with 85%+ attempt rate; if below 65%, the fix is usually not reading more but memorising the number/majority/time-limit sheet.
- **Weekly loop for a working professional:** Mon-Thu nights read 1-2 chapters each (per the hour budget above); Fri night PYQs of the week's chapters; Sat 3-4h block for the heavy chapter (Fundamental Rights, Parliament, President, Supreme Court, Centre-State Relations); Sun 1h revise the week's tables.

## Linking current affairs to this subject

A Polity news item becomes a Prelims question by stripping the event and testing the static provision behind it. Steps: (1) identify the article/Act/body in the news, (2) list its appointment-removal-tenure-powers facts, (3) note what changed (amendment, judgment, new Act), (4) frame 2-3 statements mixing one changed fact with one unchanged fact. Sources: PRS bill summary for Acts, SC judgment summary for cases, ECI/CAG press notes for bodies. Example conversions:

1. **News (2023): Parliament passes the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, replacing the SC's interim committee from Anoop Baranwal.** -> Question: statements on who appoints the CEC (President), the composition of the selection committee (PM, LoP in LS, a Union cabinet minister nominated by PM; CJI not included), and whether the removal of ECs follows the same procedure as the CEC (no: ECs are removed on the CEC's recommendation). Trap: option claiming the CJI is on the committee.
2. **News (2023): Women's Reservation (106th Amendment, Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) passed.** -> Question: which bodies get one-third reservation (LS, state assemblies, Delhi assembly; not RS or Legislative Councils), duration (15 years, extendable by Parliament), when it takes effect (after delimitation following the first census after commencement). Trap: statement that it applies to the Rajya Sabha.
3. **News (2024): Supreme Court strikes down the electoral bonds scheme (Feb 2024).** -> Question: statements on the legal basis of the scheme (Finance Act 2017 amendments to RPA Sec. 29C, Companies Act Sec. 182, RBI Act, Income Tax Act), which bank issued bonds (SBI), the ground of the verdict (voters' right to information under Art. 19(1)(a); unlimited corporate donations also struck down), and the role of the ECI (publishes the data). Trap: statement that electoral bonds were introduced by a constitutional amendment.
4. **News (2023): Supreme Court, in the Punjab Governor case, holds that a Governor cannot sit indefinitely on bills under Art. 200.** -> Question: options on Art. 200 (Governor may assent, withhold assent, return for reconsideration if not a money bill, or reserve for the President), Art. 201 (President's options; no time limit in the text), and whether the Governor must assent to a bill re-passed by the assembly (yes, unless it is reserved). Trap: statement that the Governor can return a money bill.
5. **News (2023): GNCTD (Amendment) Act enacted after the SC's May 2023 verdict on control over services in Delhi.** -> Question: Art. 239AA entries excluded from the Delhi assembly's competence (public order, police, land), the SC's position (elected government controls services except those three), what the Act created (National Capital Civil Service Authority: CM, Chief Secretary, Principal Home Secretary; LG's decision final), and which amendment created the Delhi assembly (69th, 1991). Trap: statement that Delhi is a state or that Puducherry's assembly is created by the Constitution.

Also watch the standard "news to static" pairs: a new state/UT or boundary change -> Art. 2-4 procedure; a court-ordered CBI probe -> state consent and the DSPE Act; a Speaker's disqualification order -> 10th Schedule and Kihoto Hollohan; a Finance Commission report -> Art. 280 and Art. 275 grants; a GST Council meeting -> Art. 279A voting; a President's address -> Art. 87; an ordinance re-promulgation -> Art. 123 and D.C. Wadhwa 1987; a new tribunal or its abolition -> Art. 323A/B and L. Chandra Kumar; a language added to the 8th Schedule or given classical status -> who decides and the current count.
